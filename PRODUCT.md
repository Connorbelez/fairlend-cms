# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Fairlend serves Toronto-area builders, property investors, and mortgage clients who need financing guidance for multiplex, single-family, land, acquisition, construction, permit, and completion scenarios. Visitors arrive looking for confidence that a specialist can understand the property path and move the conversation forward quickly.

## Product Purpose

The homepage introduces Fairlend as an end-to-end brokerage and investment company for real estate financing. Success above the fold means the visitor understands the lending scope, sees credible local expertise, and can begin an application without losing the premium map-led brand impression.

## Brand Personality

Precise, established, and warm. The brand should feel like a specialist lender with practical construction fluency, not a generic bank or a startup template.

## Anti-references

Avoid navy-and-gold bank cliches, generic AI finance landing pages, decorative glassmorphism for its own sake, city labels or pins that compete with the offer, cramped hero type, over-wrapped card labels, and motion that makes the map feel like a toy.

## Design Principles

1. Lead with the project path: permit, acquisition, construction, and completion should read as a guided route.
2. Keep the map useful and quiet: image, route, cards, and boats must support the financing story without visual clutter.
3. Typography carries trust: use strong hierarchy, comfortable measures, and clear financial language.
4. Forms should feel immediate: the application entry point must remain visible, compact, and easy to scan.
5. Motion must imply competent movement: reveal the page with control, animate boats in the direction they appear to sail, and respect reduced motion.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast for text and controls. Preserve keyboard focus states, semantic headings, labeled form fields, touch targets of at least 44px where practical, no horizontal scrolling, and reduced-motion alternatives for all decorative motion.
