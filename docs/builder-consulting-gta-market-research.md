# GTA builder-equation research: 2019, 2023, and 2026

Research date: July 9, 2026  
Geography: Greater Toronto Area market data; City of Toronto policy data where noted  
Currency: nominal Canadian dollars at each observation date

## Bottom line

The current section is an illustrative story, not an auditable development pro forma. There is no defensible GTA-wide numeric value for "land basis," project profit, or project margin. Those values are parcel-, program-, timing-, and financing-specific.

The current numbers should not be presented as accurate GTA market data because:

1. The visible equation omits gross buildable area, so a per-square-foot construction rate cannot be converted into a construction cost.
2. It omits closing costs, land-transfer tax, demolition, site work, design and engineering, permits, development charges, contingency, financing, carrying costs, HST treatment, and selling costs.
3. Several profit and margin values do not reconcile with each other.
4. The garden-suite row treats an accessory dwelling on the same parcel as a separately saleable home. That is not a valid generic Toronto exit assumption.
5. The section mixes GTA-wide market statistics with City of Toronto planning permissions. Four-unit multiplex and garden-suite permissions cited below are Toronto rules, not blanket rules for every GTA municipality.

The defensible solution is to either:

- present source-backed market benchmarks and label profit as site-specific; or
- present a real illustrative pro forma with every assumption disclosed and all rows calculated from one consistent formula.

### Important interpretation

The acquisition figures above are context proxies, not land values. TRREB does not publish one GTA-wide "teardown lot" average. A detached resale price includes land and an existing structure, and it varies materially by municipality, neighbourhood, lot dimensions, zoning, frontage, servicing, and development potential. It must not be added to a generic construction cost and compared with a generic new-home benchmark as if all three observations described the same property.

Likewise, BILD's new single-family benchmark includes detached, linked, semi-detached, and townhouse product (excluding stacked townhouses). It is a market benchmark, not a guaranteed exit value for a custom Toronto infill home.

## Arithmetic audit of the current implementation

The current data lives in `src/components/FairlendBuilderConsultingSection/index.tsx`, with duplicated animated counter values in `src/components/FairlendBuilderConsultingSection/Motion.client.tsx`.

If the intended equation is `profit = sale - land - build-rate x area` and no omitted costs exist, each row implies the following build area:

| Current row | Implied total cost | Implied build area after land | Actual profit / sale | Displayed margin | Result |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 2019 single-family | $3.270M | 6,386 ft2 | 10.4% | 9.8% | Does not reconcile |
| 2023 single-family | $4.062M | 3,793 ft2 | -11.3% | -10.8% | Does not reconcile |
| 2026 single-family | $4.220M | 3,795 ft2 | -14.1% | -13.1% | Does not reconcile |
| 2026 multiplex | $5.240M | 7,797 ft2 | 11.9% | 11.9% | Margin reconciles, but omitted costs remain |
| 2026 garden suite | $0.995M | 3,159 ft2 | 15.7% | 15.7% | Physically implausible as a garden-suite-only equation |

The 2023 and 2026 single-family rows appear to assume about 3,800 ft2 and no costs beyond land and hard construction. Applying the same 3,800 ft2 assumption to 2019 produces about **+$1.117M**, not **+$380K**. The 2019 row therefore contains roughly **$737K** of unexplained additional cost that is not applied consistently to the later rows.

The garden-suite row is the clearest failure: its displayed values imply $995K of cost and 3,159 ft2 at $315/ft2, while Toronto's observed median garden-suite area is about 732 ft2.

## Required data model for an auditable equation

Every scenario should calculate from numeric inputs rather than store preformatted profit and margin strings.

Minimum inputs:

- municipality, neighbourhood, and valuation date;
- actual land purchase price;
- Ontario and Toronto land-transfer tax where applicable;
- legal and closing costs;
- gross construction floor area under one stated measurement standard;
- hard-cost rate and total hard cost;
- demolition, remediation, site work, landscaping, and servicing;
- architectural, engineering, planning, survey, and consultant costs;
- permit, development-charge, parkland, and education-charge treatment;
- contingency;
- construction-loan rate, fees, draw timing, and interest;
- property tax, insurance, utilities, and other carrying costs;
- HST and rebate treatment;
- sales commissions, legal fees, warranty, and closing adjustments;
- gross sale proceeds or stabilized rental NOI/capitalization rate;
- project duration.

Recommended formulas:

```text
hard_cost = gross_floor_area_ft2 x hard_cost_per_ft2

total_development_cost =
  land_and_closing
  + demolition_and_site
  + hard_cost
  + soft_costs_and_fees
  + contingency
  + financing_and_carry
  + unrecoverable_tax
  + selling_costs

net_sale_proceeds = gross_sale_price - sale_adjustments
profit = net_sale_proceeds - total_development_cost
margin_on_revenue = profit / net_sale_proceeds
return_on_cost = profit / total_development_cost
```

Choose one margin definition, label it, and use it on every row. `profit / sale` is the most consistent with the two density rows currently displayed.

## Recommendation

Do not replace the current strings with another set of invented point estimates. Either ship the sourced market-benchmark version with `SITE-SPECIFIC` outcomes, or select one real Toronto parcel and one fixed program, disclose the complete assumptions, and calculate every state from the same numeric model. Only the second approach can support a credible profit/loss narrative.
