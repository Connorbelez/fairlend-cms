## Section 2 — Problem / Foil

**Section goal:** Name the real enemy — not "banks are slow," but the private-lending debt trap. Make the borrower feel understood and reframe speed as worthless without structure.

### Concept A — Before/After contrast columns
**Layout/aesthetic:** Two editorial columns under one bold headline. Left column ("A rushed yes") in muted/desaturated tone listing trap behaviors; right column ("A structured answer") in accent tone listing FairLend principles. A vertical divider line and matched row heights make it scan like a side-by-side ledger.

**Marketing copy:**
- Headline: **Private financing should solve pressure — not create a bigger problem.**
- Intro: Most borrowers arrive under pressure: a renewal problem, a time-sensitive closing, debt or tax pressure, bruised credit, or non-traditional income. That pressure makes a fast "yes" feel like the only thing that matters. But a private mortgage is a higher-cost tool — and speed gets expensive when the structure is hidden.
- Left ("A rushed yes"): Fees you find later · Payout penalties for leaving · Missed-payment charges that spiral · Renewal pressure that eats equity · No plan for maturity
- Right ("A structured answer"): Costs visible before signing · $0 payout fees where applicable · Missed-payment fees built for admin, not punishment · A defined exit path · Terms that match the commitment

### Concept B — Editorial pull-quote
**Layout/aesthetic:** Single-column, magazine feature style. A large pull-quote dominates, with supporting body copy in a narrower measure beside or below it. One restrained illustration or line graphic of "rate vs. true cost." Designed to slow the reader down and build trust through candor.

**Marketing copy:**
- Pull-quote: **"In private lending, speed only matters if the structure makes sense."**
- Body: The quoted rate is rarely the real story. Broker fees, lender fees, renewal fees, default charges, and payout restrictions can turn an attractive number into a misleading anchor. The question isn't "can it fund?" — it's "what does this actually cost, and how do I get out?"
- Closing line: You should understand the mortgage before you sign it.

### Concept C — Checklist of borrower rights
**Layout/aesthetic:** Bold headline over a clean, oversized checklist. Each item is a short "You should…" statement with a custom check icon. High contrast, lots of white space, list set in a confident medium weight. Reads as a borrower bill of rights.

**Marketing copy:**
- Headline: **You deserve answers before you sign — not after.**
- Checklist:
  - You should understand the true cost before signing.
  - You should know why this mortgage is suitable for *your* situation.
  - You should know what happens at maturity.
  - You should know what it costs to miss a payment, renew, or pay out.
  - You should never discover material fees in dense closing documents.
  - You should not be pushed into a structure that only solves the next few days.
