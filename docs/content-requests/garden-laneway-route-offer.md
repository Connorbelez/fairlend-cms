# Garden + laneway suite route offer

Research date: 2026-07-13

## Conversion strategy

### Audience

Southern Ontario homeowners who own a viable property, can contribute project capital, want long-term rental income, and have never managed a construction project before.

### Core customer job

Turn underused land behind an existing home into a legal, income-producing rental without having to become a developer or coordinate the property review, design, permits, builder, construction financing, draws, and takeout independently.

### Strongest selling points

1. **Create a recurring-income asset instead of taking unrestricted cash out.** The capital is used to add a self-contained rental unit and an additional income stream to the existing property.
2. **Lower the first-build barrier.** The homeowner brings the property and required contribution; FairLend coordinates the feasibility, permit, builder, construction-capital, draw, completion, and takeout workstreams.
3. **Use the completed value in the financing equation.** CMHC Improvement permits eligible financing up to 95% of the as-improved value for a 1- or 2-unit owner-occupied property. CMHC Refinance permits eligible secondary-suite financing up to 90% of the as-improved value.
4. **Reduce the monthly takeout burden.** Eligible CMHC Refinance loans may amortize for up to 30 years. The longer amortization can lower the required monthly payment relative to a shorter amortization, although it increases total interest paid. CMHC Improvement currently has a standard 25-year maximum; qualifying buyers may reach 30 years through CMHC Home Start.
5. **Model rent against debt service before construction.** The persuasive, supportable claim is that projected rent may exceed the suite's monthly takeout financing payment in a property-specific model. Positive cash flow must never be presented as automatic or guaranteed.
6. **Keep one accountable file from idea to income.** The financing plan, budget, draw timing, build scope, and takeout requirements are aligned before expensive decisions harden.

## Claim guardrails

- FairLend should evaluate both **CMHC Improvement** and **CMHC Refinance for Building Secondary Suites**. The applicable path depends on the transaction, existing mortgage, occupancy, unit count, borrower, and underwriting structure.
- CMHC Improvement permits up to 95% LTV for an eligible 1- or 2-unit owner-occupied property and up to 90% LTV for an eligible 3- or 4-unit owner-occupied property.
- CMHC Improvement currently lists a 25-year standard maximum amortization. Qualifying buyers may reach 30 years through CMHC Home Start.
- CMHC Refinance permits up to 90% LTV and up to 30-year amortization for an eligible homeowner adding a secondary suite.
- The 95% LTV and 30-year amortization claims come from different eligibility paths and must not be presented as a guaranteed combined structure.
- The existing home must already be owned by the borrower, and the borrower or a close relative must occupy one unit rent-free.
- The new unit must be legal, self-contained, compliant with applicable bylaws and regulations, and not used as a short-term rental.
- Total units after construction cannot exceed four.
- The as-improved property value must be below $2 million.
- Depending on the eligible product, financing may reach 95% loan-to-value or amortization may reach 30 years, subject to approval and all underwriting requirements.
- Additional financing is limited to construction and completion costs. Equity cash-out is not permitted under this product.
- Do not publish a rent-versus-payment example without a property-specific rent assumption, rate, amortization, mortgage amount, insurance premium, taxes, utilities, maintenance, vacancy, and contingency disclosure.
- Do not say `rent covers the mortgage`, `cash-flow positive`, `guaranteed income`, or `no equity required` without qualified, deal-specific evidence.

## Research notes

- [CMHC Refinance for Building Secondary Suites](https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/project-funding-and-mortgage-financing/mortgage-loan-insurance/mortgage-loan-insurance-homeownership-programs/refinance) confirms up to 90% LTV, an as-improved value below $2 million, up to 30-year amortization, progress advances, owner/close-relative occupancy, legal self-contained units, and no short-term-rental use.
- [Department of Finance mortgage-insurance rule changes](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2024/10/mortgage-insurance-rule-changes-to-enable-homeowners-to-add-secondary-suites.html) confirms the rules became available for insurer applications on January 15, 2025 and explicitly includes laneway homes.
- [CMHC Improvement](https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/project-funding-and-mortgage-financing/mortgage-loan-insurance/mortgage-loan-insurance-homeownership-programs/improvement) confirms insured financing up to 95% of as-improved value for eligible 1- or 2-unit owner-occupied properties and up to 90% for eligible 3- or 4-unit owner-occupied properties. It currently lists a standard maximum amortization of 25 years and points qualifying buyers to CMHC Home Start for possible 30-year amortization.
- [Ontario's laneway-house guide](https://www.ontario.ca/page/building-laneway-house) identifies rental income as a homeowner benefit and emphasizes the municipal zoning, Building Code, Fire Code, permit, and professional-coordination burden that makes FairLend's one-stop approach valuable.
- [CMHC's 2026 Mortgage Consumer Survey](https://assets.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/sites/cmhc/professional/housing-markets-data-and-research/housing-research/surveys/mortgage-consumer-surveys/survey-results-2026/mcs-2026-e-book-en.pdf) reports that 57% of respondents planning to add a secondary suite intend to collect rent, supporting rental income as the primary conversion angle.
