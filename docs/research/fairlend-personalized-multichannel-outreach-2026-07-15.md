# FairLend personalized multi-channel partnership outreach system

**Prepared:** July 15, 2026  
**Scope:** 100 account-level partnership targets: 20 builders/design/permit professionals, 20 podcasts, 20 investor/member groups, 20 recurring meetups and 20 conferences/trade events.  
**Primary objective:** create trusted, consent-based access to viable Ontario construction files *before* those files reach a general mortgage intake.  
**Primary offer:** DrawFlow—the operating layer that turns a project’s scope, budget, equity, evidence, milestones and exit into a lender-usable construction file and an intelligible draw process.

> This is a decision-and-execution document, not a mail-merge library. Every account dossier below identifies why the organization is relevant, what it has actually published or built, the value exchange to propose, what to say in each channel, what asset to send, what not to claim and the exact next step.

## Executive decision

FairLend should not run one “partnership campaign.” It should run four account-based motions with different currencies:

|---|---|---|---|
| Technical file-originators | Faster capital clarity without losing their client or scope | Capital Alignment Review + three-file DrawFlow Partner Pilot | One de-identified file review |

| Event organizers | Program relevance, attendee outcomes and sponsor revenue | Clinic/roundtable + take-home tool + curated meetings | Inventory/content qualification call |

The fastest lane to revenue is technical file-originators. Podcasts create authority and reusable content. Communities create trust at moderate scale. Events are selective channel-development bets; logo-only sponsorship should be declined.

## Offer architecture

### 1. Capital Alignment Review

A confidential 30–45 minute working session for a project professional and, with consent, the client. Inputs: location/type, current project stage, scope, total budget, sources and uses, equity already spent/available, permits/evidence, expected milestones, project liquidity and exit. Output: a one-page **Capital Alignment Memo** stating:

- information present and missing;
- whether the project is ready for a lender conversation, not an approval prediction;
- modeled draw/evidence questions;
- working-capital pressure points;
- next professional or documentation step.

**Frame:** “Protect the project from a preventable financing reset before you spend another design, permit or mobilization cycle.”

### 2. DrawFlow Partner Pilot

A 60-day pilot for up to three consented project files. FairLend provides a named contact, defined acknowledgment/status cadence, relationship protection, a readiness memo and a post-pilot review. The originating professional retains their client relationship and professional scope.

**Do not promise:** approval, funding, a rate, proceeds, a draw-release date or that documentation will be sufficient. Suitability, underwriting and disclosure remain transaction-specific.

### 3. Draw-Ready Workshop + Office Hours

A 35–45 minute case-led member session followed by five private 20-minute office hours. Core module: source/use integrity, equity timing, budget/contingency, milestone evidence, draw lag, working capital and exit. Variants:

- builder: **Can the project carry its own draw schedule?**
- broker/agent: **Five questions before sending a construction file**
- investor: **Viable project vs. executable capital plan**
- PM/asset manager: **Approved capex to draw-ready scope**
- architect/designer: **Design decisions that change construction liquidity**

### 4. Editorial package

The podcast pitch is a complete episode, not “have us on.” It includes a thesis, title options, cold open, segment rundown, two anonymized cases, opposing view, data/source pack, guest preparation, short-form clips and an interactive companion. Default episode thesis:

> A construction loan is not one cheque. Many viable projects fail at the operating seam between spending, proof, inspection and reimbursement; the useful question is whether the project can carry the draw schedule.

Sponsor fallback: if editorial fit is weak but audience fit is strong, sponsor a clearly disclosed companion calculator or listener clinic. Editorial control stays with the show.

## Personalization standard

No message is approved unless the first 80 words prove four things:

1. **Observed fact:** a named build, program, episode, article, agenda item or community practice.
2. **Interpretation:** why that fact creates a construction-capital problem or opportunity.
3. **Specific contribution:** a workshop, episode, file review, rubric or tool tailored to that fact.
4. **Small CTA:** one bounded next step with a stated agenda.

Bad: “I love what you’re doing in real estate and see synergies.”  
Good: “Your modular/panelized program puts factory deposits before conventional site milestones. I’d like to build a live file-readiness clinic around that exact cash-timing problem.”

Every account owner must re-open the cited source immediately before sending, confirm the named person still holds the role and inspect the last 30 days of public activity for a fresher hook.

## Multi-channel operating sequence

### Technical file-originator sequence

- **Day 0:** email the owner/principal with a project-specific observation and request a de-identified Capital Alignment Review.
- **Day 0:** LinkedIn connection note referencing the same build/service, not the email.
- **Day 3:** LinkedIn DM containing one useful question about when capital alignment occurs in their workflow.
- **Day 6:** phone; ask permission for a 30-second hypothesis, then two qualification questions.
- **Day 6:** voicemail if needed; reference the build and the one-page tool.
- **Day 10:** send a mock Capital Alignment Memo or relevant calculator, not “checking in.”
- **Day 17:** propose the three-file/60-day pilot with explicit operating rules.
- **Day 24:** close the loop and name a later trigger such as the next permit, acquisition or tender.

### Podcast sequence

- Email the producer/editor first with a finished episode thesis, three show-specific hooks and a listener outcome.
- Connect to the host on LinkedIn with an editorial observation; do not paste the pitch into the note.
- Day 4: send the one-page rundown and two anonymized case beats.
- Day 9: send a 60–90 second private audio/video pitch only if the show’s style makes that useful.
- Day 15: offer the guest pre-interview and source pack.
- Day 22: close editorially; only then mention the disclosed sponsor/companion fallback when appropriate.

### Community sequence

- Contact the organizer through its preferred route; reference cadence, member level and a recent program.
- Offer a pilot workshop + office hours; never ask for the member list.
- Provide organizer-ready copy, registration questions, disclosure language and post-session aggregate reporting.
- Follow up with a one-page agenda, then a sample co-branded member output.
- After the session, contact only members who separately opted in.

### Event sequence

- Qualify content, curated-meeting, attendee-consent and follow-up rights before sponsorship price.
- Prefer: learning session, hosted roundtable, clinic, webinar, app meetings or a small hospitality surface with dwell time.
- Decline: logo-only packages, unqualified badge scans and broad stages whose project scale is outside FairLend’s lane.

## Qualification and disqualification

### Account fit score (100 points)

- Sees construction decisions/files before financing: **25**
- Concentration of Ontario projects/audience within FairLend’s lane: **20**
- Public evidence of education, collaboration or specialist partnership: **15**
- Reachable owner/producer/organizer and active channel: **10**
- Concrete near-term trigger (build, season, episode theme, event date): **10**
- Measurable activation/opt-in path: **10**
- Low conflict, independence or reputational risk: **10**

Execute Tier A (75+) first; personalize within 48 hours of a current trigger. Tier B (60–74) receives a pilot/content pitch. Tier C (<60) stays in monitored research until a trigger improves fit.

### Project referral screen

Ask only enough to route a first conversation: property/project type and Ontario location, project stage, total budget/request range, equity already invested/available, permit/design state, current financing, timeline and exit. Do not request sensitive identity/credit information through LinkedIn, email or an event worksheet. Move consented prospects to the secure FairLend intake.

## Compliance and channel rules

These scripts are public-relations materials or commercial outreach and require principal-broker/compliance approval before use.

- Every public/sponsored/email asset must display the brokerage’s **FSRA-authorized name and licence number clearly and prominently**. When a broker/agent is named, use the licensed name, prescribed title and brokerage attribution.
- Never state or imply guaranteed approval, guaranteed proceeds, guaranteed timelines, “everyone qualifies,” lowest/best rates, regulatory endorsement or risk-free private mortgage outcomes.
- Do not place a rate, payment or fee in outreach. If a future advertisement does, O. Reg. 191/08 s. 18 may require APR and term at equal prominence plus a representative example.
- Referral compensation, if any, must be approved and documented under the brokerage’s policies and disclosed where required. These scripts deliberately offer member value and relationship protection—not an undisclosed bounty.
- CASL: classify each recipient and record the consent basis before sending commercial electronic messages; include sender identification and a working unsubscribe mechanism. A public email address is not blanket permission for unrelated bulk sequences. One-to-one relationship development still needs a documented basis.
- PIPEDA/privacy: collect only the minimum information needed, state the purpose, obtain consent before sharing a client/project, and never expose underwriting or borrower data in a partner portal without authorization.
- Sponsored podcast/event content must be clearly disclosed. Editorial partners keep editorial control.
- Officials, appraisers, architects and other independent professionals are offered education and clean handoffs, not inducements to influence official/professional judgment.
- Maintain the outreach, consent, source material, scripts sent, responses and transaction records in the approved system for the applicable retention period.

## Software and integration decision

The current FairLend codebase already contains most of the required primitives:

- partner intents (`partner-apply`, `partner-project`, `partner-scenario`) and a live partner page;
- Twenty CRM mirroring where partner intents create Partner Leads and project submissions create linked Construction Applications;
- signed campaign/QR attribution with scan and conversion persistence;
- consultation booking and source/campaign propagation;
- construction working-capital, draw schedule, interest carry, cost-to-complete, change-order, delay-cost, liquidity, draw-evidence, feasibility, multiplex and permit-timeline tools.

Therefore, **do not build a new CRM, calculator engine or outreach sequencer.** Configure and extend the present system in four bounded phases.

### Phase 0 — configure now, no product build

1. Create an account/campaign naming convention: `partner-{category}-{account}-{yyyy}`.
2. Assign unique intake, consultation and QR links to every activated account—not all 100 before engagement.
3. Create Partner Account records and sequences in Twenty; attach verified hook URLs and consent basis.
4. Assemble offer-specific calculator bundles from the existing catalog.
5. Store the exact approved script version sent and opt-out state.

### Phase 1 — small build after the first five positive replies

**Partner Campaign Registry.** Replace the current single hard-coded campaign configuration with a version-controlled registry or CMS collection containing campaign slug, account, motion, source, destination, active dates, disclosure variant and owner. Add validation for unique slugs and inactive redirects.

**Co-branded activation template.** One reusable route—not 100 hand-built pages—with partner/event logo permission, tailored heading, selected calculator/workshop asset, speaker/host disclosures, secure partner-project CTA and campaign attribution.

**Capital Alignment Memo generator.** Convert existing structured intake/calculator outputs into a principal-broker-approved PDF containing inputs, assumptions, missing information, modeled working-capital/draw questions, limitations and next steps. It must not output an approval score.

### Phase 2 — build only after 3 active partners or 10 consented files

**Partner referral-status portal.** This is the actual missing product. Provide consented status milestones—received, needs information, review scheduled, under review, response issued, inactive/closed—plus named next action and secure messaging. Read from Twenty/API but expose no underwriting notes, lender communications, credit data or other-client information. Every project needs explicit participant permissions, an audit log and revocation.

### Phase 3 — build when an event/podcast activation proves repeatable

- Event clinic queue and scheduled project-file office hours.
- Podcast companion pages with episode-specific calculator bundle, source pack and disclosed sponsor state.
- Aggregate partner dashboards showing consented funnel outcomes, never borrower details by default.

### No-build triggers

- Do not build the portal for verbal “interest”; require actual active partners/files.
- Do not automate LinkedIn activity or scrape member lists.
- Do not send AI-generated outreach without human source verification and compliance approval.
- Do not create integrations for a partner until the manual handoff fails at least twice or volume makes the failure predictable.

**Scope:** 20 investor/landlord organizations and 20 recurring Meetup communities in Ontario  
**Offer anchor:** FairLend's DrawFlow construction-financing program, framed as early project-file readiness, milestone-aligned capital and controlled opt-in introductions—not generic lending or a promise of approval.

## How to use this file

- Verify the named organizer, next event and member count on the linked first-party page immediately before sending. Meetup counts and event calendars move frequently.
- Personalize the first two sentences from the evidence hooks below; never claim to have attended an event that FairLend did not attend.
- Ask for a small programming test: one educational session, five opt-in file reviews, and an aggregate outcome report. Do not ask for a member list.
- Any reference to qualification, timing or funding is subject to FairLend underwriting, complete documentation, property/project eligibility and legal/compliance review. Never promise approval, funding, rate, valuation or turnaround.

## Core member offer

**Draw-Ready Project Clinic:** a 30–45 minute worked Ontario case covering scope, plans/permits, sources and uses, contingency, borrower equity, milestone evidence, working-capital gap and exit/takeout. Attendees receive a neutral Draw-Ready File Checklist and calculator. Up to five members can opt into a confidential 15-minute fit screen. The organizer receives only aggregate outcomes unless the member explicitly consents to an introduction.

## Cross-community execution guardrails

1. **Sequence:** Day 0 email + LinkedIn note; Day 3 platform/contact-form message; Day 7 phone/voicemail; Day 12 follow-up with the one-page abstract; Day 21 final value-add follow-up; Day 30 close-loop. Stop immediately on a “no.”
2. **Consent:** organizers never transmit member data. The member submits FairLend's co-branded form and explicitly consents to contact and organizer-visible status, if any.
3. **Reporting:** share registrations, attendance, worksheet completion, opt-in screens, complete files, fit/no-fit and next-step counts in aggregate. Never share underwriting reasons or personal information with organizers without explicit consent.
4. **Commercials:** education first. Discuss membership/sponsorship only after topic and audience fit; disclose every paid relationship. No referral fee or member discount before legal/compliance review.
5. **Stale leads:** M13, M14, M19 and M20 require a reactivation check before any spend; Peterborough and NNLA require current calendar/board verification. Meetup organizer names, counts and dates must be checked on send day.
6. **Claims:** say “fit screen,” “subject to review” and “may be eligible.” Never say approved, guaranteed, fastest, cheapest, risk-free, endorsed or preferred unless documented and currently true.
