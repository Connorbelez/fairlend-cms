# FairLend / DrawFlow speaker-opportunity discovery brief

**Prepared:** July 28, 2026  
**Primary speaker:** Elie Soberano  
**Purpose:** Find net-new, current opportunities where Elie can teach builders and real-estate operators, earn trust, and create qualified FairLend / DrawFlow relationships.  
**Operating mode:** Research only. Do not contact, submit, register, pitch, message, or apply on FairLend's behalf.

## 1. Decision and target

This is not a generic event scrape. The agent must find organizations and event series with:

1. a concentrated audience of people who originate, influence, fund, refer, or execute construction and renovation projects;
2. an education surface where an external practitioner can credibly teach;
3. current evidence that the organization or event is active;
4. a real route to the organizer, programming team, chapter leader, education committee, sponsorship team, or speaker application; and
5. a specific reason Elie's construction-finance and DrawFlow material improves the audience's decisions.

The default research batch is **120 net-new, qualified speaker opportunities**. Quality outranks quota. If a lane cannot support its target without weak or stale leads, return the verified shortfall and the rejected candidates instead of padding the list.

## 2. Geographic and timing parameters

### Geographic tiers

1. **Tier 1 — GTA and Greater Golden Horseshoe:** Toronto, York, Peel, Halton, Durham, Hamilton, Niagara, and adjacent markets. Search deeply.
2. **Tier 2 — Southern and Central Ontario:** Waterloo Region, Guelph/Wellington, Barrie/Simcoe, London, Windsor, Peterborough, Kingston, and comparable markets.
3. **Tier 3 — Rest of Ontario:** Include when the audience, speaking access, or repeatability is strong.
4. **Tier 4 — Canada-wide:** Include national virtual programs, Ontario-accessible programs, and flagship Canadian events that justify travel.
5. **Outside Canada:** Exclude by default. Include only when the event has material Canadian/Ontario attendance, a remote speaker format, or exceptional strategic value. Explain the exception.

Record the actual city, province, venue, time zone, and in-person/virtual/hybrid format. Do not infer that an online community is local merely because its name contains a city.

### Time horizon

- Search date: use the agent run date and record it.
- One-time events: prioritize the next **18 months**.
- Annual events: require a currently announced next edition, a live call for speakers/prospectus, or strong evidence that planning is active.
- Recurring groups: require an upcoming session or public activity within the previous **180 days**.
- Evergreen webinar or workshop series: require a current registration page, current calendar, current host activity, or a recently delivered edition.
- Past-only events are not qualified. They may be retained in the rejection log if the organizer is strategically useful but recurrence is unverified.
- Capture every known programming, speaker, sponsorship, early-bird, and submission deadline using an absolute date and source.

## 3. Audience parameters

### Primary audiences

- custom-home, infill, missing-middle, garden-suite, laneway-suite, multiplex, and small/mid-sized residential builders;
- renovators, general contractors, construction managers, and owner-builders;
- house flippers, BRRRR operators, active real-estate investors, small developers, and value-add landlords;
- short-term-rental and Airbnb buyers/operators planning acquisition, conversion, or renovation;
- professionals who repeatedly see construction files before financing is finalized.

### Secondary audiences

- mortgage brokers, private-lending professionals, real-estate agents, brokerage owners, and referral teams;
- architects, designers, planners, engineers, quantity surveyors, appraisers, building consultants, building officials, inspectors, and permit professionals;
- construction suppliers, lumberyards, manufacturers, dealer networks, restoration firms, and property managers;
- real-estate lawyers, accountants, insurers, and advisors serving active investors or builders.

### Audience disqualifiers

Exclude or heavily penalize:

- stock, crypto, forex, or generic wealth events with no real-estate operator concentration;
- first-time-homebuyer events with no renovation, suite, redevelopment, or investor angle;
- generic networking with no evidence an educational presentation is possible;
- consumer home shows where the only access is a vendor booth and the audience has little project-finance intent;
- luxury or commercial events whose project profile is materially outside FairLend's Ontario construction-finance lane;
- communities built mainly to sell an opaque coaching program, with no independent evidence of active members or programming;
- United States-only groups with no Canadian relevance;
- events whose audience, organizer, date, or current activity cannot be verified.

## 5. What constitutes a real speaking opportunity

Classify the speaking-access evidence:

| Access class | Evidence |
|---|---|
| `A_OPEN_APPLICATION` | Current call for speakers, presenter application, session-proposal form, or published submission instructions |
| `B_PROGRAM_CONTACT` | Named programming chair, education committee, conference producer, chapter education lead, or official programming email |
| `C_PROVEN_GUEST_FORMAT` | Current/recent agenda shows external guest experts, panels, workshops, webinars, or member education |
| `D_SPONSOR_EDUCATION` | Prospectus or sponsor package includes a workshop, stage, webinar, panel, demo, or educational session |
| `E_ORGANIZER_ROUTE_ONLY` | Active, relevant program with an official organizer contact but no explicit evidence of external speakers |
| `F_UNVERIFIED` | No verifiable presentation path; do not qualify for the main list |

Access classes A–D are preferred. Class E can qualify only with strong audience fit, current activity, and a credible personalized programming hypothesis. Class F belongs in the rejection or watchlist file.

Do not confuse a paid exhibit booth with a speaking opportunity. If access appears pay-to-play, identify the package, price if public, the educational inventory, and whether the session is editorially controlled or sponsor-controlled.

## 7. Educational presentation architecture

The discovery agent is not writing a sales deck. It must select the strongest educational hypothesis for each opportunity and explain why the audience would value it.

Every proposed session must follow this structure:

1. **Teach the operating problem:** show how project scope, budget, permits, sequencing, evidence, draws, interest exposure, contingency, and exit planning interact.
2. **Give the audience a usable tool:** checklist, worksheet, draw-readiness scorecard, sample timeline, anonymized case, or decision framework.
3. **Demonstrate with evidence:** use an anonymized build timeline, draw overlays, interest-exposure graph, or before/after file structure when approved.
4. **Introduce DrawFlow only as the operating method:** connect the lesson to better milestone planning and evidence, without turning the session into a product pitch.
5. **Use a low-friction CTA:** optional file review, office hours, co-branded workshop, organizer debrief, or access to the educational worksheet.

For qualified candidates, inspect enough current material to understand:

- what the organization teaches;
- who its members actually are;
- recent and recurring themes;
- past speaker roles and session formats;
- explicit member problems;
- the organizer's stated mandate;
- sponsor or partner restrictions;
- whether financing content has already been covered and, if so, what is missing;
- which DrawFlow lesson would add new educational value.

Read available agendas, recaps, blogs, newsletters, speaker pages, public recordings, and public social posts. For video or audio, use published transcripts when available; otherwise transcribe only the relevant segments and record the source and time range. Do not claim to have consumed inaccessible content.

### Phase 4 — Deduplicate, score, and rank

Normalize candidates before scoring. One organization may generate multiple records only when the opportunities are materially different, such as:

- a national conference and a recurring Ontario chapter webinar;
- a trade show and a separately programmed year-round education series;
- distinct regional chapters with different organizers and programming.

Do not create separate leads for duplicate event listings, ticket platforms, dates in the same annual series, or spelling variants.

### Phase 5 — Produce research artifacts

Produce:

1. a human-readable Markdown report;

3. a CSV suitable for review and import mapping;
4. a rejection/watchlist appendix with explicit reasons; and

## 10. Evidence requirements

Every qualified record must include:

1. **Canonical opportunity source:** official organization, event, chapter, or program page.
2. **Recency source:** current calendar, next edition, recent session, registration page, or official announcement.
3. **Speaking-access source:** call for speakers, agenda, past-speaker archive, prospectus, program contact, or equivalent evidence.
4. **Audience evidence:** explicit audience description, member mandate, agenda, session topics, or credible attendance/member data.
5. **Contact route:** official form, role inbox, programming contact, organizer profile, or named public business contact.

Use at least two distinct source URLs per record and preferably three. One URL can satisfy more than one requirement only when the page genuinely contains the evidence.

For each source, store:

- URL;
- page title;
- publisher/organization;
- accessed/verified date;
- what it proves;
- date of the underlying item;
- whether it is primary, official-social, platform, or secondary evidence.

Contact data rules:

- Never guess an email address.
- Never infer a person's role from an unverified profile.
- Prefer official role inboxes and public business contact details.
- Label contact confidence `VERIFIED`, `ROLE_ROUTE_ONLY`, or `UNVERIFIED`.
- `UNVERIFIED` contacts cannot be promoted as primary outreach routes.

## 11. Qualification and scoring

Score every validated candidate out of 100.

| Dimension | Points | Standard |
|---|---:|---|
| Audience concentration and commercial fit | 0–25 | Builders, active investors, flippers, STR buyers/operators, or high-value early-file referrers are demonstrably present |
| Educational and DrawFlow problem fit | 0–20 | Project planning, construction, renovation, financing, evidence, draws, cash flow, permits, or exit planning are relevant |
| Speaking access and openness | 0–20 | Current application, programming contact, external speaker history, workshop inventory, or credible organizer route |
| Timing, recency, and deadline usability | 0–15 | Upcoming/current, enough lead time, and evidence verified |
| Audience reach and influence | 0–8 | Attendance, membership, chapters, subscribers, repeat cadence, or decision-maker density |
| Geographic and delivery fit | 0–5 | Ontario/GTA access, virtual reach, or justified national opportunity |
| Organizer contactability | 0–5 | Named or role-based official route with a practical next step |
| Repeatability and partnership potential | 0–2 | Recurring sessions, chapters, co-branded education, or ongoing member benefit |
| **Total** | **100** |  |

### Penalties

- Past-only or stale evidence: `-15` to `-35`
- No speaking mechanism and no credible organizer route: `-20`
- Audience is broad but weakly concentrated: `-10` to `-20`
- Paid booth mistaken for educational access: `-20`
- Opaque lead seller, coaching funnel, or unverifiable membership: `-10` to `-30`
- Material geography/logistics mismatch: `-10`
- Duplicate of an existing CRM/research lead: disqualify as net-new
- Duplicate listing of the same opportunity: merge, do not score separately

### Tiers

- `TIER_A` — 75–100: prioritize for speaker outreach.
- `TIER_B` — 60–74: qualified; needs one material confirmation or a longer-cycle approach.
- `WATCHLIST` — 45–59: plausible but insufficiently open/current; do not count toward 120.
- `REJECTED` — below 45 or any hard disqualifier.

The final 120 must all score 60 or higher. The top 30 should normally include a class A–D speaking route.
