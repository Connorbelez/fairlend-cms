# Garden Suite Appraisal, Rent, Takeout, and Risk FAQs

These answers address the completed property and the risks that can interrupt the route from construction financing to a longer-term mortgage.

## FAQ 01: Can projected Garden Suite rent help me qualify?

Sometimes. Lender treatment varies by program, property, borrower, suite status, appraisal method, market-rent support, and lease evidence.

Projected rent is evidence, not guaranteed qualifying income. Ask how much the selected lender may recognize, what expenses or vacancy assumptions it uses, and what documentation it needs.

## FAQ 02: Why might a lender require an appraisal?

An appraisal provides an independent opinion of value and may also address the proposed improvements or supported market rent. The lender uses it with other information to assess collateral, loan-to-value, project feasibility, and the exit.

The appraiser does not approve the mortgage, and the construction cost does not determine the appraised value.

## FAQ 03: What is an as-complete appraisal?

An as-complete appraisal estimates the property's market value after the proposed legal work is finished, based on the plans, specifications, market evidence, and appraisal assumptions.

The lender may require updates or final confirmation during or after construction. A projected value can change if the scope, market, legality, quality, or completion status changes.

## FAQ 04: Will a Garden Suite increase my property value?

It may affect value, but no responsible adviser can guarantee the amount. The result depends on legality, design, size, utility, rent potential, operating costs, buyer demand, property context, market conditions, and the appraiser's evidence.

Plan the project using a conservative range and a financing route that does not depend on an unsupported value increase.

## FAQ 05: What is a takeout mortgage?

A takeout mortgage is the longer-term financing intended to replace construction or bridge financing after the project reaches the required completion stage.

It may have a different lender, rate, term, amortization, qualification test, appraisal requirement, and document package. The takeout should be modelled before construction begins.

## FAQ 06: Why plan the takeout before the build?

The construction facility may be short-term and may require repayment by a specific date. If the borrower cannot qualify for the expected takeout, the project may face extension fees, a forced refinance, additional equity requirements, or sale pressure.

Planning early tests whether the projected debt, income, rent, value, and completion date support a realistic exit.

## FAQ 07: What evidence can be required at completion?

The lender may require final or progress inspections, permit and occupancy evidence, proof of completed work, updated appraisal, invoices, statutory declarations, insurance, title or lien material, deficiency information, leases or rent support, and confirmation of the final debt.

The exact list depends on the construction facility and takeout lender. Track it before the last draw.

## FAQ 08: Can CMHC-insured refinancing help fund a Garden Suite?

Potentially. CMHC currently offers a refinance product for eligible homeowners creating a self-contained secondary suite. Current criteria include owner or close-relative occupancy of a unit, limits on property value and loan-to-value, credit and debt-service requirements, eligible project costs, and progress advances.

The suite cannot be used as a short-term rental under the product. A lender must still approve the mortgage and submit it for insurance.

## FAQ 09: Does CMHC Refinance require owner occupancy?

Yes. CMHC's current product says the borrower must own the home and the borrower or a qualifying close relative must occupy at least one unit on a rent-free basis.

Confirm the live criteria and relationship definitions before relying on the product. Other lenders and insurers may have different programs and requirements.

## FAQ 10: Can the suite be used as a short-term rental?

Toronto's zoning rules and the selected financing product both matter. CMHC Refinance states that the secondary suite cannot be used as a short-term rental and defines that restriction using rental periods shorter than 90 consecutive days.

Do not use projected short-term-rental income in a financing plan unless the use is legal and expressly accepted by the lender and insurer.

## FAQ 11: What happens if the appraisal is lower than expected?

A lower value can reduce available credit, increase the required homeowner contribution, weaken the planned takeout, or require a different financing route.

Review the appraisal, scope, completion assumptions, comparable evidence, and lender policy. Do not assume the result can be changed. Rebuild the capital plan around the value the lender will accept.

## FAQ 12: What happens if construction costs exceed the budget?

Update the remaining-cost forecast immediately. Identify approved changes, committed costs, unpaid work, available contingency, undrawn credit, homeowner funds, and the minimum amount needed to reach a financeable completion stage.

Do not use future rent or value as if it were cash available today. The project may need a scope decision, additional equity, lender approval, new financing, or a formal restructuring.

## FAQ 13: What if I cannot qualify for the planned takeout mortgage?

Start with the reason. The constraint may be income, debt service, credit, value, rent treatment, incomplete work, permit status, documentation, or product eligibility.

Possible responses can include reducing debt, adding verified income or liquidity, resolving deficiencies, changing lenders or products, extending construction financing, or selling. Every option has cost and approval risk, so act before the construction facility matures.

## FAQ 14: Can FairLend help if my project has stalled?

FairLend can review the current debt, title, permits, work completed, remaining budget, builder status, liens, appraisal, available capital, and intended exit to determine whether a financing route may be available.

That review does not guarantee a rescue. Some projects can be restructured, while others need more equity, a reduced scope, a negotiated settlement, or another professional response.

## FAQ 15: What reduces Garden Suite financing risk?

The strongest controls are early site review, a complete and current budget, realistic contingency, clear contracts, qualified professionals, aligned draw milestones, verified evidence, adequate working capital, conservative value and rent assumptions, and a takeout plan tested before construction.

Risk cannot be removed, but it can be made visible early enough to manage.
