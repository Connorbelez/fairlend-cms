## Recommended on-page set

A large landing-page FAQ should usually expose 10 to 14 questions. A strong first release for `/toronto-garden-suite-financing-gta` is:

1. What is Garden Suite financing?
2. Can FairLend help before I have plans or permits?
3. How do I know whether my Toronto property can support a Garden Suite?
4. Do I need a building permit?
5. Do Toronto's pre-approved plans guarantee permit approval?
6. How much equity do I need?
7. Can I use a HELOC or refinance?
8. What documents will a lender ask for?
9. How do construction draws work?
10. Why does the draw schedule need to match the build schedule?
11. Can projected rent help me qualify?
12. What happens after construction is complete?
13. Is the Canada Secondary Suite Loan Program available?
14. Can FairLend help if construction has started or funding has stalled?

Use short answers in the accordion and link to deeper page sections when a visitor needs calculations, regulatory detail, or a document checklist.

## Copy rules

- Use direct, plain-language answers. Lead with the answer, then explain the condition.
- Never promise approval, a rate, a timeline, a valuation result, or a specific amount of available credit.
- Keep municipal approval, lender approval, appraisal conclusions, and FairLend's advisory role distinct.
- Treat government programs, fees, zoning rules, and mortgage-insurance parameters as dated facts.
- Do not imply that a pre-approved building design removes site-specific zoning, access, servicing, tree, or permit review.
- Do not describe projected rent as automatically qualifying income.
- Do not describe construction progress reviews as municipal or building-code inspections.
- Do not publish internal proof claims marked `HOLD` in the source ledger.
- Avoid em dashes and en dashes in final page copy.

## Voice

The reader is often interested but uncertain. They may have a property and a goal but no reliable budget, permit path, or financing sequence. The copy should make the next decision clearer without pretending the entire project can be approved from a web page.

FairLend's strongest message is coordination: property facts, permits, budget, construction milestones, financing, and the final mortgage belong in one project plan.

## Source hierarchy

1. Current City of Toronto, Ontario, Canada, CMHC, and FSRA sources for public rules and programs.
2. Executed lender agreements, product documentation, and approved FairLend evidence for company-specific claims.
3. The current landing-page implementation and approved SEO synthesis for message continuity.
4. General editorial guidance only when no factual claim is involved.
