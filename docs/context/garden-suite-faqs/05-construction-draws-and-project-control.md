# Garden Suite Construction Draws and Project Control FAQs

These answers explain how a staged financing facility can follow construction progress. Company-specific DrawFlow terms remain subject to the source ledger.

## FAQ 01: What is a construction draw?

A construction draw is an advance from an approved financing facility during the build. The request is usually tied to completed work, eligible costs, supporting evidence, and the conditions in the loan agreement.

A draw is not extra credit added to the facility. It is the release of part of the approved amount.

## FAQ 02: Why do construction lenders use progress draws?

Progress draws connect financing to the value and work created during construction. They help the lender verify that the project is advancing and that enough capital remains to complete it.

For the homeowner, staged advances can reduce the amount borrowed before it is needed, but they also require disciplined evidence, timing, and working capital.

## FAQ 03: What is a construction milestone?

A milestone is a defined stage or deliverable in the build, such as an approved scope of completed work supported by invoices, photos, reports, or an inspection.

Useful milestones match the actual construction sequence and payment obligations. Vague milestones make it difficult to know when a draw can be requested or what evidence is required.

## FAQ 04: Who requests a draw?

The borrower remains responsible for the loan and generally authorizes or submits the request through the process established by the lender and administrator. A builder may prepare progress information, invoices, and a proposed payment request.

The exact authority and workflow must be stated in the executed agreement. No page copy should imply that a builder can borrow against the homeowner's facility without the required authorization.

## FAQ 05: What evidence can be required for a draw?

Evidence may include a draw request, invoices, proof of payment, statutory declarations, progress photos, site reports, inspection or quantity-surveyor material, permit information, insurance, lien-related documents, and an updated cost-to-complete budget.

Requirements vary by facility and milestone. The evidence list should be known before the related work is scheduled.

## FAQ 06: Why should the draw schedule match the construction schedule?

The builder needs funds when deposits, labour, trades, and materials become payable. The lender releases funds when its milestone and evidence conditions are met.

If those timelines do not match, the project can face a cash gap even when the total approved credit appears sufficient. Align payment dates, milestones, document preparation, review time, and working capital before construction begins.

## FAQ 07: Do I pay interest on the full approved construction facility?

Not always. Some staged facilities charge interest on amounts after they are advanced, while fees, minimum interest, standby costs, or other charges may apply to the approved or unused amount.

The executed agreement controls. Ask for a dated advance schedule and cost model instead of relying on a general statement that interest applies only to money used.

## FAQ 08: Can DrawFlow provide up to 15 draws?

FairLend's current product copy describes access to up to 15 milestone-based draws. The actual number, size, timing, conditions, and availability must be confirmed in the approved facility and lender agreement.

Use this claim publicly only after product and legal review confirm the wording in the source ledger.

## FAQ 09: What is DrawFlow?

DrawFlow is FairLend's name for a coordinated construction-financing and project-control process. The intended model connects the approved credit facility, milestones, evidence, draw requests, schedule, budget, and project participants in one workflow.

The software does not replace the builder, municipal inspection, legal advice, appraisal, or lender approval. It organizes the information those roles need.

## FAQ 10: Who can see a DrawFlow project?

The current product design uses role-based access for homeowners, builders, contractors, suppliers, lenders, and FairLend. Each participant should see only the project information and actions appropriate to that role.

Access, privacy, document retention, and permissions must follow the live product configuration and agreement. Do not expose borrower, lender, or project-sensitive information in marketing screenshots.

## FAQ 11: Are FairLend progress reviews the same as City inspections?

No. Municipal officials perform the inspections and approvals required under the permit and building-code process.

FairLend or its project professionals may review progress and evidence for financing, schedule, budget, or milestone purposes. That review does not replace a City inspection or certify code compliance unless a qualified person is separately engaged and authorized to provide that service.

## FAQ 12: What happens when the scope or cost changes?

Record the proposed change, price, schedule effect, reason, approval, and impact on remaining contingency before the work proceeds where possible. Update the cost-to-complete forecast and draw plan.

The lender or administrator may need to approve material changes. A change that is affordable in isolation can still create a completion shortfall when combined with other changes.

## FAQ 13: How does the builder get paid between draws?

Payment depends on the construction contract, homeowner funds, supplier terms, and lender advance process. Some costs may be paid from working capital and reimbursed after evidence review. Others may be paid directly after a draw.

The contract and financing plan should describe deposits, progress billings, holdbacks, taxes, evidence, review periods, and what happens if a draw is delayed or reduced.

## FAQ 14: What can cause a construction draw to be delayed?

Common causes include incomplete evidence, work that does not match the milestone, unresolved permit or insurance conditions, budget changes, liens or title issues, appraisal or inspection delays, an expired document, or concern that the remaining funds cannot complete the project.

The fastest response is a complete issue list with owners and next actions. Repeatedly submitting the same incomplete request usually adds delay.

## FAQ 15: Can a project switch financing after construction has started?

Sometimes, but it can be harder. A new lender may need to review title, existing debt, completed work, permits, budget, remaining cost, builder, liens, appraisal, insurance, and a credible completion and exit plan.

Some files can be restructured and some cannot. The earlier the shortfall is identified, the more options may remain.
