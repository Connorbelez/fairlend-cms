## 1. Executive Summary

The call converged on a clearer marketing-page strategy:

1. **Define FairLend more objectively before selling the vision.**  
   The “Who We Are” section should start with what FairLend technically is: an **FSRA-licensed mortgage brokerage and administrator** specializing in **private lending and construction financing**.

2. **Avoid over-hyping the team.**  
   “Old school meets new school” is a useful internal framing, but public-facing copy should avoid sounding self-congratulatory. The copy should describe the practical value created by combining seasoned mortgage judgment with modern technology, automation, and clear workflows.

3. **Keep the language simple.**  
   The target reading level should be plain, direct, and close to grade 8. Avoid terms like **SLA**, “workflow orchestration,” “hybrid automation stack,” “Zapier-style,” and other technical jargon.

4. **Builder/construction support is a major differentiator.**  
   The team agreed that the construction/building section can go deeper than the other sections because construction financing is one of FairLend’s strongest differentiators.

5. **The current transition into the builder section feels abrupt.**  
   The “What We Finance” section currently jumps too suddenly into a construction-heavy pitch. The team wants a smoother bridge while still preserving the strength of the builder-consulting narrative.

6. **The interactive builder equation should be simplified.**  
   The equation should focus on the core economics builders actually care about: **land price, build cost, soft costs, sale value, and profit margin**. It should compare old single-family luxury-home economics against newer multiplex economics.

7. **Core story for the equation section:**  
   The old single-family luxury-home math used to work, but by around 2023 it stopped working. Multiplex opportunities, combined with government incentives and better financing structures, now change the equation.

## 2. Global Messaging Principles

### 2.1 Objective facts first, ethos second

The team wanted the homepage to move from concrete facts to broader ethos:

1. Start with objective descriptors.
2. Then explain the practical specialization.
3. Then communicate the operating philosophy.
4. Only then talk about team identity, technology, fairness, and the broader mission.

Recommended structure:

```text
What FairLend is → What FairLend specializes in → How FairLend works → Why that matters
```

### 2.2 Do not lead with vague “digital era” claims

The phrase “bringing private mortgages into the digital era” was considered too hand-wavy when used too early. The page should first say exactly what FairLend does.

Better direction:

```text
FairLend is an FSRA-licensed mortgage brokerage and administrator specializing in private lending and construction financing.
```

Then layer on:

```text
We combine experienced mortgage judgment with modern technology, automation, and clear processes.
```

### 2.3 Avoid self-congratulatory copy

The team explicitly flagged that the “Who We Are” section should not feel like it is simply praising the company or team.

Avoid:

- “We are changing the industry.”
- “Old school meets new school” as the entire public-facing message.
- Too much emphasis on how impressive the team is before the visitor understands what FairLend actually does.
- Tech-stack language that sounds impressive to engineers but meaningless to borrowers, builders, or lenders.

Use instead:

- Plain description of the business.
- Concrete specialization.
- Clear practical benefit.
- Human expertise plus modern process.
- Fair treatment of all parties.

### 2.4 Keep wording accessible

The team repeatedly emphasized that builders and broader visitors should not need specialized vocabulary to understand the page.

Rules:

- Use short sentences.
- Avoid finance and software jargon unless necessary.
- Replace acronyms with plain-language equivalents.
- Prefer “help,” “guide,” “process,” “financing,” “support,” and “clear” over technical or abstract terms.
- Write for a practical builder, borrower, or investor who wants to know whether FairLend can help them.

## 7. Interactive Builder Economics / Equation Section

### 7.1 Purpose

This section should show that FairLend understands the economic reality builders are facing.

Core idea:

```text
The construction equation has changed. Builders who used to make money on single-family luxury homes now need a new model. Multiplexes, incentives, and better financing structures can make the numbers work again.
```

The section is not meant to be a full underwriting model. It is meant to:

- Signal domain expertise.
- Make experienced builders feel understood.
- Show that FairLend knows the real economics.
- Create an “if you know, you know” reaction.
- Lead into FairLend’s guidance and leadership.

### 7.2 Key narrative

1. Historically, luxury single-family development followed a simple formula.
2. Land and construction costs were roughly balanced.
3. Sale values supported a healthy profit margin.
4. Over time, land and build costs rose.
5. By around 2023, the old luxury-home equation stopped working.
6. By 2025/2026, multiplex opportunities and incentives changed the game.
7. Builders now need help structuring the new equation.

### 7.3 Approved / preferred headings

Initial state / problem heading:

```text
The old luxury home math stopped working.
```

Solution heading:

```text
Multiplex opportunities change the game.
```

Additional supporting line:

```text
Let us guide you through the process.
```

### 7.4 Recommended variable set

Reduce the equation to the variables builders actually care about.

| Variable | Meaning | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Land price | Cost to acquire the land/property | Core input. |
| Build cost | Hard construction cost | Should move historically with construction inflation. |
| Soft costs | Financing, carrying costs, permits, development charges, professional costs | Needs tooltip or small caption. |
| Sale price / value | Finished value or expected sale value | Use “value” if project may be held/refinanced. |
| Profit margin | Resulting margin | The key emotional/business outcome. |

Remove or avoid excessive boxes such as:

- Fit score.
- Too many financing subcomponents.
- Too many abstract variables.
- Project outcome as a separate box unless visually useful.
- Variables that do not directly matter to the builder’s immediate economics.

### 7.5 Row structure

Several options were discussed. The strongest direction:

1. Start with one row for **single-family luxury home economics**.
2. Animate through historical checkpoints.
3. When the single-family row goes negative, introduce the **multiplex** row.
4. End with a clear side-by-side comparison:
   - Single-family: negative or weak margin.
   - Multiplex: positive margin.

Final-state visual should allow the visitor to compare both rows without remembering prior numbers.

Recommended layout:

| Row | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Single-family luxury home | Shows the old model becoming unprofitable. |
| Multiplex | Shows the new model becoming profitable after incentives/rule changes. |

### 7.6 Timeline checkpoints
