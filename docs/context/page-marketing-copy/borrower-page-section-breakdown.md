# Borrower Page Section Breakdown

**Page:** Private Mortgage Financing for Borrowers
**Audience:** Ontario homeowners and property owners considering private mortgage, bridge, renewal, second mortgage, debt consolidation, or equity-based financing
**Goal:** Get qualified borrowers to request a free consultation with enough trust that they expect a clear answer, transparent terms, and an exit plan, not a rushed approval promise
**Source:** `docs/context/mortgage-financing-page-content-plan.md`
**Register:** Brand marketing surface
**Visual system:** FairLend Editorial Topographic Grid, warm paper, ink linework, visible rails, electric lime action cues, high-trust serif display type

> Compliance note: Every public claim below marked `[COMPLIANCE]` must be cleared before publishing. The 3-day commitment must remain a target and must be qualified by file completeness, underwriting, property review, appraisal requirements, borrower cooperation, available capital, and lender fit. The $50 missed-payment fee and any $450 market comparison can publish only after the fee schedule and benchmark are substantiated. Founder stats such as 29+ years and $2B+ funded need exact approved wording. Avoid guaranteed approval, guaranteed funding, guaranteed refinance, guaranteed savings, "lowest rate," "risk free," "bad credit approved," "no questions asked," and any claim that FairLend removes all borrower risk.

## Strategic Shape

This page should not look or read like a generic "private money fast" page. The sharper foil is the private-lending debt trap: fees hidden outside the commitment, payout restrictions, punitive missed-payment charges, weak suitability work, renewal pressure, inflated values, and no credible path out at maturity.

The page thesis:

> Private mortgage financing should be fast enough to solve pressure, clear enough to understand before signing, and structured enough that the borrower knows the way out.

The emotional promise is relief without being trapped. The rational promise is a structured private mortgage review that looks at borrower capacity, property value, current mortgage position, available equity, documentation, timing, total cost, fee exposure, payout terms, renewal path, and exit strategy together.

## Design Brief

**Primary user action:** Request a free consultation.

**Secondary user action:** Speak with a mortgage specialist, especially for borrowers who are stressed, embarrassed, or under deadline pressure.

**Color strategy:** Restrained. Warm paper, ink, fine grid lines, and lime only for conversion, active state, progress, and selected markers.

**Theme scene sentence:** A stressed Ontario homeowner reads this at a kitchen table after a bank renewal problem or closing deadline, in daylight, with paperwork open and enough anxiety that calm precision matters more than visual spectacle.

**Anchor references:**

- FairLend `DESIGN.md`: editorial finance grid, topographic routing, pen-and-ink local real-estate illustration.
- FSRA-style consumer protection clarity: suitability, disclosure, material risks, and exit planning translated into plain borrower language.
- Architectural field-note discipline: paper, rails, annotated file elements, measured lines, no fintech gloss.

**Recommended visual lane:** Editorial borrower-protection dossier. The page feels like a clear financing file being opened in front of the borrower: commitment terms, property value, payout path, maturity plan, and servicing all made visible. Use document fragments, grid ledgers, route lines, annotated commitments, residential line art, and topographic contour details. Do not use generic smiling homeowner stock photography.

**Image direction:** Use monochrome line illustration and UI-native document/property diagrams. Good candidates are a GTA residential home, a commitment sheet, a property value field note, a mortgage maturity route, a kitchen-table paperwork vignette, or a quiet Toronto/Ontario residential street. Keep all core copy semantic, not rasterized.

**Motion direction:** Precise and restrained. Section rules can reveal, route lines can draw gently, FAQ rows can expand, and CTA arrows can nudge. Avoid bounce, parallax clutter, fee-stat theatrics, or anything that makes the borrower feel rushed.

## Page Flow

1. Hero: clear offer, CTAs, proof strip, compliance qualifier.
2. Problem / Foil: private financing can create a bigger problem when structure is hidden.
3. Solution: FairLend's fairer structure and whole-file review.
4. Borrower Scenarios: help visitors self-identify without promising approval.
5. Differentiators: the anti-trap spine.
6. How It Works: five clear steps from consultation to closing and administration.
7. Trust / Proof: local mortgage judgment, founder credibility, administration capability.
8. FAQ / Objections: answer the hard questions plainly.
9. Final CTA: consultation form and low-pressure close.

- `clear answer`
- `transparent terms`
- `full picture`

- `what happens at maturity`

If the costs, risks, payment plan, payout rules, renewal mechanics, default charges, and exit are not clear, speed can become expensive.

**Will the legal documents include fees that were not in the commitment?**

The borrower should know the material mortgage economics before closing. The commitment should disclose rate, fees, term, payment structure, payout terms, renewal considerations, default charges, material conditions, and material risks. Third-party government, registration, legal, appraisal, and closing costs may still apply where relevant.

**What is an exit strategy?**

An exit strategy is the realistic plan for what happens at maturity: refinance, sale, renewal, income stabilization, credit repair, debt cleanup, or another defined path. It should be discussed before funding, not when the term is about to expire.

**What happens when the term ends?**

FairLend plans for maturity from the beginning and can coordinate renewal or payout handling where applicable. The right path depends on your file, market conditions, payment history, documentation, valuation, and exit readiness.
