## Section 2 — Answer block and homeowner reassurance

## How can you finance a Garden Suite in Toronto?

A Toronto Garden Suite may be financed with available equity, a mortgage refinance, HELOC, second mortgage, construction financing or a staged combination. The right route depends on your property, current mortgage, borrower profile, plans, permit stage, budget, contingency, appraisal evidence, draw timing and repayment plan. Every option requires lender-specific review.

- **Use existing equity:** refinance, HELOC or second-mortgage structures.
- **Match construction cash flow:** plan milestones, deposits, working capital and reimbursement draws.
- **Support the completed project:** provide plans, cost-to-complete, appraisal or rent evidence, and a takeout strategy where required.

### You do not need to be a builder to start a Garden Suite project

Start with your property, current mortgage, goal and preliminary budget. FairLend then helps you:

1. **Build the capital plan:** equity, financing, contingency, working capital and repayment or takeout.

## Section 5 — Financing-route comparison

## Compare Garden Suite financing options

Most Toronto Garden Suite projects use one of five routes — or a staged combination. Compared on equivalent assumptions, there is no universal winner; the right route depends on your property, existing mortgage, project stage and repayment plan.

| Route                   | May fit when                                                                   | Evidence commonly needed                                                               | Construction cash-flow issue                                          | Key limitation to disclose                                                                              |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mortgage refinance      | Sufficient equity and a longer-term restructuring makes sense                  | Income, credit, property value, current mortgage, project purpose                      | Often a lump sum, not construction-stage controls                     | Break costs, new terms, qualification and total borrowing cost require review                           |
| HELOC                   | Accessible equity and flexible staged spending                                 | Property value, secured debt, borrower qualification                                   | Limit may not cover the full project                                  | Variable-rate exposure, qualification and available-limit constraints                                   |
| Second mortgage         | First mortgage should stay in place, or conventional options do not fit timing | Equity, title/mortgage position, repayment and exit plan                               | Can bridge deposits, working capital or a defined shortfall           | Typically higher cost and fees; suitability and exit must be explicit                                   |
| Construction financing  | Staged advances tied to completed work                                         | Plans, permits, budget, contracts, appraisal, equity, draw schedule, borrower evidence | Advances may reimburse completed stages, requiring borrower liquidity | Inspection, holdback, timing, contingency and lender criteria vary                                      |
| Verified public program | A current program is open and the project and borrower satisfy its rules       | Program-specific eligibility and evidence                                              | Program timing may not match construction cash flow                   | Availability and eligibility can change; an announced or closed program is never presented as available |

Combinations may be considered; suitability, terms and availability are always file-specific. ## Section 6 — Cost-to-capital bridge

## Your construction budget is not the same as your financing requirement

A build estimate is only part of the capital your project needs. The full stack:

1. Design, consultants and feasibility work.
2. Municipal applications, permits and other verified charges.
3. Site preparation, demolition and access.
4. Utility and servicing work.
5. Hard construction costs.
6. Contingency tied to project risk — not an arbitrary leftover.
7. Financing, appraisal, legal, inspection, carrying and draw costs where applicable.
8. Lease-up, occupancy and ongoing debt-service requirements.

The financing question is where each dollar comes from and when: **total project cost → borrower/equity contribution → available financing → timing gap**. That gap is where under-planned projects run out of cash between reimbursement draws — and what a DrawFlow schedule is built to make visible.

For the full cost picture, read [Toronto Garden Suite costs and feasibility](/resources/garden-suite-cost-toronto).

## Section 7 — How lenders assess the project

## What lenders review for Garden Suite financing

Lender review is evidence-driven, across five areas:

1. **Borrower:** income, credit, debts, liquidity, experience where relevant and ability to carry the proposed obligations.
2. **Property:** ownership, existing mortgage, value evidence, title/security position and property-specific constraints.
3. **Project:** Garden vs. Laneway type, plans, permit/design stage, scope, contracts and delivery team.
4. **Budget and draws:** complete budget, contingency, deposits, cost-to-complete, draw schedule and working-capital capacity.
5. **Completion and repayment:** appraisal assumptions, rental/occupancy plan, takeout or long-term debt-service path and material risks.

A document's presence in a file is not verification, suitability or approval. FairLend's job is to assemble this evidence so a lender sees a complete, coherent project — and to tell you honestly when the evidence is not there yet.

## Section 8 — Appraisal and projected rent

## Can projected Garden Suite rent help you qualify?

Sometimes — but never automatically.

- A lender or insurer may consider some rental-income evidence under its current policy.
- Treatment varies by lender, property, suite status, appraisal methodology, lease evidence and borrower profile.
- Proposed rent is not automatically accepted as qualifying income.
- An appraisal or market-rent opinion is evidence, not a guarantee of lender treatment or completed value.
- No universal percentage of rent is accepted across lenders; any specific program rule must be current, cited and reviewed before it appears here.

For the rental economics of the project, see [Toronto Garden Suite costs and feasibility](/resources/garden-suite-cost-toronto).

## Section 9 — Process and timing

## From feasibility to funded construction draws

Six stages, in order:

1. **Project screen:** property, municipality, project type, preliminary feasibility, intended use.
2. **Capital plan:** complete budget, contingency, equity contribution, required financing.
3. **Evidence package:** plans, permit stage, borrower documents, mortgage/property evidence, quotes/contracts, insurance needs.
4. **Appraisal and lender assessment:** current and as-complete value and rent evidence where required; lender-specific underwriting.
5. **Closing and advances:** deposits, borrower contribution, conditions, inspections, draw sequence.
6. **Completion and long-term structure:** occupancy/completion evidence, takeout or refinance where applicable, sustainable ongoing payments.

We do not publish guaranteed turnaround times. Every stage has municipal, appraisal, lender and construction dependencies outside any brokerage's control.

## Garden Suite and Laneway Suite financing share a page — not identical project facts

## Section 12 — Failure modes and rescue intent

## What commonly stops or delays Garden Suite financing?

Most files fail on one of these — not on the idea itself:

- Existing secured debt leaves insufficient usable equity.
- Property feasibility or permit stage is assumed rather than evidenced.
- The budget is not tied to scope, servicing or site constraints.
- Contingency is too thin for the project stage.
- Projected rent or completed value is unsupported.
- Borrower liquidity cannot bridge deposits or reimbursement draws.
- The selected lender or product does not fit the property, timing or project stage.
- Construction has started and cost-to-complete is no longer reconciled.
- The exit or takeout plan does not support the interim structure.

Finding out which applies is the point of an assessment — cheaper before a deposit is due than after.

### Already facing a draw gap, lender decline or project deadline?

An urgent review starts from where the file stands: mortgage position, work completed, remaining budget, permits, liens or claims where relevant, available equity, required timing and plausible exit. Some files can be restructured; some cannot. We will tell you which you have — without implying a rescue is always possible.

## Section 14 — FAQ

## Garden Suite financing questions

**1. How can I finance a Garden Suite in Toronto?**
Through home equity, a refinance, HELOC, second mortgage, construction financing or a staged combination. The right route depends on the property, current mortgage, borrower profile, plans, permit stage, budget, appraisal evidence, draw timing and repayment plan. Every option requires lender-specific review.

**2. What is DrawFlow, and how does it work for a Garden Suite?**
DrawFlow matches construction capital to verified milestones. Your schedule can include up to 15 draws with borrower-controlled request timing. FairLend reviews the evidence and may approve and release funds under the lender agreement when the conditions are satisfied, without ad hoc lender approval for each release.

**3. Does every DrawFlow facility support up to 15 draws and borrower-controlled timing?**
Yes. Every facility supports up to 15 milestone-based draws; you or your builder leads the schedule and chooses when to request funds within available capacity. FairLend retains release authority, but each draw still depends on its milestone, evidence and file-condition review.

**4. How do FairLend's case studies support the interest-savings claims?**
Multiple case studies compare builder-led DrawFlow schedules with a disclosed fixed three-draw baseline. The highest demonstrated construction-period interest saving is up to 50%. The approximately $12,000 result is a separate worked illustration under published assumptions — not a quote, approval or guaranteed saving. Individual results vary.

**5. Can I use a HELOC or refinance to build a Garden Suite?**
Possibly. A refinance may suit sufficient equity and longer-term restructuring; a HELOC may suit flexible staged spending. Both require borrower and property qualification. Consider refinance break costs and new terms, or a HELOC's variable rate and available limit. Neither automatically covers the full project.

**6. Can projected Garden Suite rent be used for mortgage qualification?**
Sometimes. Treatment varies by lender, property, suite status, appraisal method, lease evidence and borrower profile. Proposed rent is not automatically qualifying income, and an appraisal or market-rent opinion does not guarantee lender treatment or completed value.

**7. How much equity and working capital do I need?**
There is no universal amount. Equity depends on property value, secured debt, requested financing and lender criteria. Working capital depends on deposits and how long you must carry completed work before reimbursement. The assessment maps your contribution, available financing and timing gap.

**8. Do I need permits before applying for construction financing?**
Not to start a review. Your design and permit stage determines what evidence a lender needs and when. Starting at feasibility or design stage can settle the capital plan, equity position and draw structure earlier. Assumed permit approval is never treated as evidence.

**9. What if costs rise or a draw is delayed?**
Plan for contingency, deposits and reimbursement timing before construction. Where DrawFlow applies, cost-to-complete and documentation monitoring can surface issues earlier. Already facing a gap, decline or deadline? Request an urgent review. Some files can be restructured; rescue is never guaranteed.

**10. Can FairLend finance a Laneway Suite or review a lender decline?**
Yes. Laneway Suite financing uses similar routes, but lane access, servicing, permit evidence and appraisal assumptions change the file. A decline review starts with your mortgage position, equity, remaining budget, timing and plausible exit, then assesses whether a viable structure exists.
