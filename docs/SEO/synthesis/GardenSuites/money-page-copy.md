## DrawFlow combines the credit line and project software

DrawFlow is FairLend's construction financing program for staged builds. It combines a milestone-based line of credit with in-house software that helps FairLend and authorized project participants plan the build, coordinate evidence, monitor progress and manage draws.

The line of credit can be accessed through up to 15 borrower-controlled milestone draws. The software connects four parts of the program:

1. **Fund:** Match release timing to verified milestones, evidence and approved line-of-credit terms.
2. **Plan:** Convert scope, budget, dependencies and cash requirements into a construction and draw plan.
3. **Coordinate:** Give homeowners, builders, contractors, lenders and other authorized project participants a shared workspace.
4. **Adapt:** Recalculate downstream milestones, cash requirements and draw plans when actual progress differs from plan.

## Keep builders funded without drawing the full line of credit upfront

DrawFlow coordinates the construction schedule with the release schedule. Builders and trades can plan around the next milestone draw and its evidence requirements, while homeowners draw only the capital the project is ready to use.

**For the builder and trades**

- Funding is planned around actual deposits, materials, labour and milestone requirements.
- Up to 15 draws can reduce the pressure created by a rigid small number of large advances.
- The shared plan and evidence requirements reduce ambiguity around the next release.
- Adaptive scheduling helps prevent one delayed sub-milestone from jamming the entire build.

**For the homeowner**

- The line of credit is drawn as the project reaches verified milestones and needs the capital.
- Interest accrues on advanced capital rather than the full approved line before it is drawn, subject to the loan agreement and fee structure.
- DrawFlow tracks daily burn, cash on hand and upcoming obligations.
- You can see progress, funding status and next requirements at any time.

| Route | Best fit | Capital timing | Interest exposure | Typical evidence focus | Key limitation |

| Refinance | Sufficient equity and supportable mortgage | Upfront | Often on full funded balance | Property, income, appraisal | Break costs and premature borrowing |
| Second mortgage | Gap or speed-sensitive capital | Usually upfront | On advanced balance | Equity and exit | Higher carrying cost/fees may apply |
| Government programs (verify current status) | Eligible projects where a current program applies | Set by the program | Set by the program | Program eligibility and documentation | Availability, terms and eligibility change; never assume qualification |
| DrawFlow milestone-based line of credit | Staged project with verifiable milestones | Up to 15 borrower-controlled draws | On advanced capital | Budget, team, progress and evidence | Setup and evidence workflow take longer than a simple upfront advance; best suited to staged builds, not small funding gaps |

## Laneway Suite financing in Toronto

Laneway Suite financing uses many of the same routes as Garden Suite financing, but the project facts differ — and lenders review project facts.

- A Garden Suite is a detached ancillary dwelling on the same lot as the principal residence; it is not defined by public-lane access.
- A Laneway Suite depends on the lane relationship and its planning and access requirements.
- Access, servicing, construction staging and appraisal context may change the budget and financing plan.
- The financing method may overlap, but the project evidence and site constraints can differ.

## Garden Suite financing questions

**1. Can FairLend help if I do not have a builder yet?**
Yes. Start with the property, budget and goal. FairLend helps identify the team your project requires and connects you to builders, contractors and suppliers from its established network. Introductions are optional. See [the project journey](#section-3).

**2. Can FairLend help with planning and permits?**
FairLend supports the planning and permit pathway and connects you to planning and permit professionals. It does not replace municipal authorities, architects or engineers — approvals remain the municipality's decision. See [the project journey](#section-3).

**3. How does FairLend help me find builders, contractors and suppliers?**
Through its established network of planning professionals, builders, general contractors, trades, suppliers, appraisers, legal professionals and capital providers. FairLend explains how participants are identified and any relationships involved; you choose who to engage. See [the network](#section-11).

**4. What does FairLend's in-house legal team do?**
The specialist legal team, with 10+ years of experience, works on FairLend's agreements, lender authority and project structure so risk is considered early. It does not act as your lawyer; independent legal advice remains yours. See [the team](#section-4).

**5. What is DrawFlow?**
DrawFlow is FairLend's construction financing program: a milestone-based line of credit with up to 15 borrower-controlled draws, supported by in-house software that connects the build schedule, cash plan, progress evidence and draw workflow. See [DrawFlow](#section-5).

**6. How does the DrawFlow software create and update the construction schedule?**
It converts scope, budget and dependencies into sub-milestones, analyzes the dependency graph, and recalculates downstream milestones, cash requirements and draw plans when actual progress differs from plan. See [the software](#section-6).

**7. What does "AI trained on thousands of Toronto builds" mean?**
DrawFlow's schedule and cost estimation use AI trained on thousands of Toronto builds, combined with evidence-backed construction heuristics and dependency-graph analysis. `[PLACEHOLDER: DrawFlow product/data owner — approved public dataset definition and methodology description]` See [the software](#section-6).

**8. Can I request my own construction draws?**
Yes. Draw timing is borrower-controlled: you or your builder decide when to submit a request within the approved line of credit and evidence requirements. FairLend reviews each request against the release conditions. See [funding terms and release authority](#drawflow-claims-methodology).

**9. How many draws can I receive?**
A DrawFlow line of credit can be accessed through up to 15 borrower-controlled milestone draws. See [funding terms and methodology](#drawflow-claims-methodology).

**10. Who decides when funds are released?**
FairLend has authority under the lender agreement to review, approve and release funds when the milestone, evidence and file conditions are satisfied. The lender does not give ad hoc permission for each release. See [release authority](#drawflow-claims-methodology).

**11. How can more precise draws reduce interest?**
You pay interest only on released capital. Drawing when the project needs funds — rather than borrowing the full budget on day one — limits interest to outstanding funded capital. Case studies demonstrate up to 50% construction-period interest savings. See [the evidence](#case-ledger).

**12. How does DrawFlow keep builders and trades funded?**
Funding is planned around deposits, materials, labour and verified milestones. Up to 15 draws, shared evidence requirements and adaptive scheduling help the team anticipate upcoming cash needs and adjust when work moves off plan. See [capital efficiency](#section-7).

**13. Who can access the DrawFlow workspace?**
Homeowners, builders, contractors, suppliers, lenders and FairLend can use the client portal. Access is role-based, so each participant sees and acts on the project information their role requires. See [the workspace](#section-9).

**14. What is captured by daily drone footage and weekly inspections?**
Daily drone footage records site progress; weekly walkthroughs and progress inspections review the work; milestone reviews attach evidence to the corresponding DrawFlow milestone. These are FairLend progress inspections, distinct from municipal inspections. See [field oversight](#section-10).

**15. Can I use a HELOC or refinance instead?**
Possibly — both use existing equity and may fit smaller or earlier costs. A HELOC's limit may not cover a full build, and a refinance often means interest on the full balance upfront. See [the comparison](#section-13).

**16. Can projected rent help me qualify?**
Sometimes, but never automatically. Treatment varies by lender, property, suite status, appraisal methodology and lease evidence. An appraisal or market-rent opinion is evidence, not a guarantee. See [appraisal and rent](#section-15).

**17. What happens after construction is complete?**
FairLend reviews completion evidence, appraisal or rent support and outstanding deficiencies before moving to the takeout mortgage planned before construction began. See [takeout planning](#section-15).

**18. Can FairLend plan for CMHC-insured financing?**
Yes — FairLend plans the qualification path using current program sources, where the project may be eligible. CMHC eligibility and insurance decisions remain CMHC's and the lender's. See [CMHC planning](#section-15).

**19. What is different about Laneway Suite financing?**
The financing routes overlap, but a Laneway Suite depends on the lane relationship, access and servicing — which can change budget, staging, appraisal context and required evidence. See [Laneway Suite financing](#section-16).

**20. Can FairLend help if my project has already stalled?**
Yes — stalled draws, cost overruns, appraisal gaps and completion shortfalls are exactly what an urgent review covers. Some files can be restructured; some cannot. See [failure modes](#section-18) or use the urgent CTA.

**21. Is there a federal loan program for building a Garden Suite?**
Federal secondary-suite loan programs have existed, but availability, terms and eligibility change. Do not assume qualification from an announcement. For current, dated program status, see [Toronto Garden Suite costs and feasibility](/resources/garden-suite-cost-toronto). `[PLACEHOLDER: mortgage/compliance — last-reviewed date]`

**22. Does Toronto offer forgivable loans or incentives for Garden Suites?**
City incentive and forgivable-loan programs open, close and change. As of the last review, check current status before planning around any program. Dated coverage: [Toronto Garden Suite costs and feasibility](/resources/garden-suite-cost-toronto). `[PLACEHOLDER: mortgage/compliance — last-reviewed date]`
