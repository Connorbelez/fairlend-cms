# FairLend OpenSEO trial research contract

**Status:** Approved research brief; execution has not started  
**Decision date:** 2026-07-16  
**Target market:** Toronto-first, GTA-secondary  
**Research stack:** OpenSEO + DataForSEO + NotFair research/briefing workflows  
**Paid-data budget:** US$1 included balance; US$0.90 hard execution ceiling  
**Site/CMS authority:** Read-only  
**Stopping point:** Content-ready research briefs; no marketing copy or implementation

## 1. Objective

Use a tightly budgeted OpenSEO trial to validate a repeatable research workflow for attracting qualified GTA financing opportunities involving:

1. five-plus-unit multiplex acquisition, construction, stabilization, and takeout;
2. garden and laneway-suite construction financing;
3. builder construction cash flow and FairLend's DrawFlow program; and
4. B2B referral relationships with mortgage brokers, realtors, and builders.

The trial must identify and prioritize the searches, questions, personas, financing triggers, competitors, page opportunities, and source evidence required to create the right content later. It must not create that content now.

The durable unit of analysis is a **page opportunity**, not an isolated keyword.

## 2. Primary commercial outcome

The primary conversion is a **qualified project-financing assessment** from a prospect who:

- owns or has identified a GTA property;
- has credible equity or down-payment capital;
- intends to acquire, convert, or build within 12 months;
- intends to create income-producing units;
- needs acquisition, construction, bridge, rescue, or takeout financing; and
- is willing to provide documentation and undergo underwriting.

Raw traffic, newsletter subscriptions, and unqualified general mortgage enquiries are not primary success measures.

## 3. Strategic positioning

The research will test and support this positioning:

> FairLend helps GTA property owners, builders, and small-scale developers turn suitable properties into income-producing multi-unit housing, coordinating financing and professional support from acquisition through construction and long-term takeout, with a strong preference for five-plus-unit, MLI Select-compatible projects.

FairLend operates as an end-to-end project partner and coordinated point of contact. It can coordinate its professional network, but future content must not imply that FairLend directly performs regulated or specialist architectural, planning, legal, tax, appraisal, construction, or CMHC functions when those services are delivered by partners.

All financing, program eligibility, timelines, approvals, savings, and outcomes remain subject to underwriting, third-party decisions, project facts, and applicable program rules.

## 4. Commercial weighting

### 4.1 Core vertical weighting

- **60% multiplex financing**
- **40% garden/laneway-suite financing**

Within the multiplex allocation, five-plus-unit, build-to-hold, MLI Select-compatible projects receive dominant weighting. Two-to-four-unit multiplexes remain a secondary feeder segment and must be analyzed separately because their underwriting, program eligibility, takeout path, and search intent differ.

### 4.2 Default exit strategy

Build-to-hold and stabilized rental ownership are the default commercial assumptions. Build-to-sell, condominium, and co-ownership opportunities remain in the persona universe but receive lower priority unless live evidence shows exceptional qualified demand.

### 4.3 Toronto and GTA weighting

- Toronto is the primary market and the default location for live SERP sampling.
- GTA-wide financing terms form the secondary geographic layer.
- Surrounding municipalities may be promoted only where demand, local rules, or SERP composition justify a genuinely distinct opportunity.
- The research must not propose thin city-name substitution pages.

## 5. Trial workstreams

### Workstream A — Five-plus-unit multiplex and MLI Select

Prioritize:

- five-plus-unit acquisition and development;
- build-to-hold rental projects;
- construction-to-takeout planning;
- MLI Select-compatible planning and insured takeout searches;
- capital stack, equity, liquidity, experience, documentation, and feasibility;
- affordability, energy efficiency, accessibility, projected rents, stabilization, and program-readiness questions;
- government incentives and official federal, Ontario, and Toronto program searches;
- property purchasers assessing an opportunity before waiving conditions;
- first-time and experienced small-scale developers;
- landlords and property owners converting suitable assets; and
- bank-declined but potentially viable projects.

Government programs, incentives, rebates, tax measures, and MLI Select requirements must use current official sources, include jurisdiction and verification dates, and carry a freshness/review requirement.

### Workstream B — Garden and laneway-suite financing

Primary persona:

> A GTA homeowner with usable property equity who wants to build a legal garden or laneway suite primarily for stable long-term rental income.

Secondary branches:

- multigenerational housing;
- alternatives to short-term rental/Airbnb income;
- bank-declined or conventionally constrained financing;
- HELOC versus second mortgage versus construction financing;
- garden suite versus basement apartment;
- renovate versus add a suite versus sell;
- cost, rent, payback, feasibility, approval, and financing readiness; and
- long-term versus short-term rental economics.

### Workstream C — DrawFlow and builder financing

Builders seeking financing are a primary commercial/B2B group.

Research the language and demand around:

- construction lines of credit;
- milestone-based or progressive availability;
- on-demand draws following completed-and-verified work;
- borrower-controlled draw timing;
- funding deposits, materials, trades, HVAC, windows, kitchens, and other costs before conventional reimbursement draws;
- rigid draw schedules;
- inspection and reimbursement delays;
- idle borrowed capital and interest drag;
- overlapping projects and working-capital constraints;
- stalled draws, lender replacement, budget overruns, and partially completed projects; and
- construction financing that reflects actual project cash flow.

Distressed/stalled projects are an actively targeted, high-intent rescue cluster with strict qualification. They are not the primary public positioning.

#### DrawFlow claim status

FairLend states that case studies support up to 50% interest savings. Those case studies are not currently available for review.

For this trial:

- the DrawFlow product mechanics and customer problems may be researched;
- the existence of supporting cases may be recorded as a user-supplied assertion;
- the 50% figure must be marked **provisional/unverified**;
- the figure must not be presented as an approved public claim or incorporated into future content-ready messaging until underlying cases, calculations, comparison assumptions, limitations, and compliance approval are available;
- prohibited interpretations include guaranteed savings, universal 50% savings, unconditional draws, or guaranteed prevention of project cash-flow problems.

The research package must include an anonymized case-study evidence template for later completion.

### Workstream D — B2B partner/referral acquisition

Primary B2B groups:

1. mortgage brokers with construction, multiplex, alternative-financing, or bank-declined files;
2. realtors identifying acquisition/development opportunities or clients needing financing strategy; and
3. builders actively seeking acquisition, construction, DrawFlow, rescue, or takeout financing.

Secondary terminology samples:

- architects;
- planners;
- contractors and trades;
- appraisers;
- property managers; and
- other professionals who encounter projects early and can produce legitimate referrals or authoritative collaboration.

Partner intent must remain separate from borrower-facing intent. Referral partners are a secondary portfolio; direct qualified borrower/developer leads remain the primary KPI.

## 6. Persona priorities

### 6.1 Tier 1 multiplex personas

1. Homeowner/citizen developer unlocking property value.
2. Landlord converting an existing GTA property.
3. Bank-declined or conventionally non-bankable borrower with a viable project.
4. Property purchaser evaluating a multiplex opportunity.
5. Build-to-hold developer pursuing MLI Select or another long-term takeout.
6. Builder seeking flexible construction financing or DrawFlow.

### 6.2 Tier 2 multiplex personas

Retain and classify, but do not weight equally unless live evidence supports promotion:

- single-family builder moving into multiplex construction;
- first-time small-scale developer;
- experienced rental investor moving into development;
- BRRRR/value-add investor moving into major construction;
- build-to-sell developer;
- owner-occupier/house hacker;
- multigenerational family developer;
- landowner partnering with a builder/investor;
- capital-constrained multi-project builder;
- borrower experiencing construction-draw cash-flow problems;
- stalled or partially completed project owner;
- modular/prefabricated developer; and
- non-profit/community/affordable-housing sponsor.

The trial may discover adjacent personas. The supplied list is a seed model, not a closed ontology.

## 7. Project-lifecycle classification

Every persona, query cluster, and page opportunity must map to one or more stages:

1. Property identification and acquisition financing.
2. Feasibility, capital-stack, and takeout planning.
3. Pre-construction approvals and readiness.
4. Construction financing and draw management.
5. Lease-up and stabilization.
6. MLI Select or other institutional takeout.
7. Rescue, refinance, or lender-replacement scenarios.

## 8. Funnel classification

Preserve two headline categories while using five operational stages.

### Research / thought-leadership opportunity

1. **Awareness and education** — definitions, programs, concepts, and basic options.
2. **Property/project feasibility** — cost, eligibility, zoning/process, equity, rent, unit count, and viability.
3. **Planning and solution comparison** — financing paths, alternatives, professionals, timelines, and trade-offs.

### Buying signals / lead-capture opportunity

4. **Financing qualification and lender comparison** — requirements, products, terms, lender selection, applications, and takeout strategy.
5. **Immediate transaction or problem** — identified property, bank decline, stopped draws, cash-flow gap, stalled project, acquisition deadline, or lender replacement.

Each opportunity must receive a funnel stage, confidence level, intended CTA, and qualification expectations.

## 9. Opportunity scoring

Do not collapse all value into one score. Produce two independently ranked portfolios.

### 9.1 Lead Capture Score

Recommended 100-point model:

- 25: buying intent and urgency;
- 20: fit with the qualified-lead definition;
- 15: five-plus-unit/DrawFlow/garden-suite strategic fit;
- 10: Toronto/GTA applicability;
- 10: commercial evidence such as CPC/paid competition;
- 10: organic attainability and SERP weakness;
- 5: conversion-path clarity; and
- 5: evidence confidence.

### 9.2 Authority Build Score

Recommended 100-point model:

- 20: recurring research demand;
- 15: topical importance to the financing journey;
- 15: question/AI-answer citation opportunity;
- 15: ability to demonstrate original FairLend or partner expertise;
- 10: internal-link value to a commercial page;
- 10: link/reference worthiness;
- 10: SERP attainability; and
- 5: evidence confidence.

Scores must show their inputs and must not hide missing data behind false precision.

## 10. Traffic and topic exclusions

Exclude or heavily down-rank broad traffic unless it clearly connects to the defined project-financing outcome:

- generic bad-credit mortgages;
- generic second mortgages;
- generic private lenders;
- ordinary home-purchase mortgages;
- homes/apartments for rent;
- garden design and landscaping;
- Airbnb property management;
- generic DIY construction;
- jobs, courses, and certifications;
- tenant-only questions; and
- unrelated consumer renovation loans.

High search volume does not override project relevance.

## 11. Research method

### 11.1 OpenSEO role

Use OpenSEO as the canonical research and state layer for:

- keyword expansion;
- live keyword metrics;
- intent, CPC, competition, difficulty, and monthly trends where available;
- Toronto-targeted SERP samples;
- ranked-domain and SERP-competitor discovery;
- local/business-result observations where the budget permits;
- current-site mapping and audit evidence; and
- later GSC integration when first-party data exists.

### 11.2 NotFair role

Use NotFair as the methodology and research-brief layer for:

- intent classification;
- keyword clustering;
- content/page planning;
- cannibalization checks;
- metadata/schema/internal-link requirements;
- GEO/AI-answer readiness requirements;
- source and claim requirements; and
- content-ready briefs.

NotFair must not write final page or article copy during this run.

### 11.3 Mixed-source evidence

The paid quantitative backbone may be supplemented with:

- the FairLend website and supplied SEO audit;
- the approved SEO/operator playbook;
- Google Search Console if useful data exists;
- live public search observations;
- official CMHC, federal, Ontario, Toronto, and municipal sources;
- People Also Ask, related searches, and autocomplete;
- public Reddit/forum language as qualitative question evidence;
- competitor, partner, and authority pages; and
- current search-engine/platform documentation.

Label every source type. Do not mix official program facts, estimated keyword metrics, qualitative community language, or model-generated prompts as though they are equivalent evidence.

## 12. Competitor model

Analyze three classes:

1. **Commercial competitors:** GTA financing providers competing for the lead.
2. **Search competitors:** pages actually ranking, including lenders, brokers, builders, architects, municipalities, CMHC, banks, and publishers.
3. **Authority competitors:** sources earning citations, backlinks, featured snippets, People Also Ask visibility, or AI-answer mentions.

Competitor-name pages are not automatically recommended. The trial must distinguish competitive research from a justified public comparison opportunity.

## 13. AI and question-discovery layer

AI discoverability is critical and receives a separate evidence layer covering:

- People Also Ask and related searches;
- natural-language questions and objections;
- Reddit/forum terminology;
- video-result intent where present;
- queries triggering answer-heavy or AI Overview-style SERPs;
- Bing/ChatGPT-style prompts; and
- original facts, passages, tables, examples, or expert evidence needed for FairLend to become a citable source.

Synthetic AI prompts must not be reported as keywords with fabricated search volume.

The website is the canonical source. The trial may identify derivative opportunities for Google Business Profile, Reddit, YouTube, partner publications, downloadable assets, email, and tools, but it must not plan or write those campaigns.

## 14. Page-architecture rule

Treat the information architecture below the homepage as greenfield.

- Preserve the homepage as the core brand/positioning surface, but allow research-backed metadata, pathway, internal-link, CTA, and trust recommendations.
- Evaluate existing subordinate pages only for reusable facts, claims, components, and potentially useful URLs.
- Let persona intent and SERP evidence determine the proper hubs, money pages, supporting resources, and page boundaries.
- Preserve an existing URL only when it matches the winning intent.
- Otherwise, propose replacement, consolidation, retirement, and eventual redirect logic.
- Proposals only; no changes in this stage.

A new page is justified by distinct intent and SERP evidence, not by a lexical keyword variation.

## 15. Conversion mapping

Recommended stage-matched conversions:

- Research pages: **Assess my property/project** or a useful feasibility tool/checklist.
- Financing/comparison pages: **Get a financing strategy** with a short qualification form.
- Urgent/problem pages: **Submit my project for review** with property, capital, timeline, budget, and current financing problem.
- Partner pages: **Refer or discuss a project** with role, client/project stage, location, capital need, and urgency.

The primary conversion remains a project-financing assessment. Research briefs may specify the CTA and form information but may not write final CTA copy.

## 16. Evidence-acquisition register

Every page opportunity must list the source material required before content creation, including as applicable:

- project examples or anonymized case patterns;
- underwriting criteria and disqualifiers;
- financing products and process details;
- acquisition/construction/takeout sequencing;
- draw structures and failure modes;
- MLI Select planning constraints;
- program eligibility and current official sources;
- partner expertise and named accountable reviewers;
- calculations, checklists, diagrams, timelines, or decision frameworks;
- testimonials or case studies with documented permission;
- quantitative-claim substantiation; and
- legal, compliance, tax, securities, or Principal Broker review requirements.

Missing evidence must remain missing. The agent must never invent it.

## 17. Automation and publication boundaries

This stage is strictly read-only against:

- the live website;
- Payload CMS;
- source code and templates;
- Google Business Profile and other business listings;
- analytics/search-platform settings; and
- any third-party account or external person.

The system may create only the approved research artifacts in the output directory.

It must not:

- create or update Payload drafts;
- modify repository source files;
- publish pages or posts;
- change metadata, schema, redirects, navigation, or internal links;
- edit business listings;
- contact partners, prospects, publications, or forum users;
- generate final copy; or
- spend beyond the stated paid-data ceiling.

Future page additions/changes must be delivered as proposal documents and require explicit human authorization. Even after authorization, financial-services content requires Principal Broker/compliance approval before publication.

## 18. Paid-data budget governor

The account's included US$1 balance is the complete trial budget.

- Hard execution ceiling: **US$0.90**.
- Reserve: **US$0.10** for rounding, delayed charging, or an unavoidable verification call.
- Log every paid task, endpoint, task count, estimated cost, and returned cost if exposed.
- Prefer batched keyword endpoints and a deliberately small set of representative SERPs.
- Do not add Semrush, Ahrefs, or another paid subscription.
- Stop before exceeding the ceiling.
- If a provider cannot expose or safely predict cost, stop and report the blocker rather than guessing.

The trial validates the workflow on a representative sample. It does not pretend to be an exhaustive market study.

## 20. Forecasting policy

Use scenario ranges, not promises:

- addressable demand range;
- click opportunity adjusted for SERP composition;
- ranking-horizon band;
- expected lead-quality tier;
- conservative/base/upside conversion assumptions;
- evidence confidence; and
- dependencies.

Do not manufacture precise traffic, ranking, lead, or revenue forecasts for a new domain.

## 21. Trial success gates

The trial succeeds only if it demonstrates all of the following within budget:

1. Live OpenSEO/DataForSEO keyword evidence is retrieved and cost-logged.
2. Each of the four workstreams produces at least one defensible cluster.
3. Keywords are classified by persona, trigger, lifecycle, geography, and funnel stage.
4. Lead Capture and Authority Build scores remain separate and auditable.
5. A small live SERP sample changes or validates at least one clustering/page decision.
6. Commercial, search, and authority competitors are distinguished.
7. AI/question opportunities are recorded separately from search-volume keywords.
8. Greenfield page opportunities are mapped without writing content.
9. Required source evidence and compliance gates are explicit.
10. The DrawFlow 50% claim remains provisional pending case-study review.
11. No website, CMS, repository source, platform, or external-account mutation occurs.
12. The package estimates the cost and scope of a full production research run.

## 22. Recommended defaults for remaining decisions

Unless live evidence forces a documented exception:

- language: English;
- country/data market: Canada;
- primary location: Toronto;
- device: mobile-first SERP sampling with desktop only when materially different;
- freshness: current live data, with 12–24 months of trend history where the endpoint provides it economically;
- safe search and ordinary organic results: default provider settings appropriate to Canadian business research;
- keyword expansion: broad enough to discover unknown adjacent opportunities, but filtered by project relevance;
- page boundaries: SERP/intent overlap over lexical similarity;
- official program claims: primary sources only;
- community evidence: qualitative language/questions only, not factual authority;
- rankings: localized observations, never universal claims;
- trial depth: representative, not exhaustive;
- generated prose: prohibited;
- mutations: prohibited; and
- ambiguous financial claims: exclude or flag for human review.
