# Requirements brief — Toronto multiplex feasibility and 2–4 versus 5+ boundary

**Status:** proposal only; not finished copy  
**Priority:** P2  
**Proposed canonical path:** `/resources/multiplex-feasibility-toronto/`

## Search task

Help a homeowner, purchaser, landlord, builder, or first-time developer decide whether a Toronto property opportunity belongs on a 2–4-unit conversion/new-build path or a 5+ unit development/takeout path, and identify the evidence needed before committing capital.

## Evidence

- The exact feasibility portfolio returned no numeric rows for the most specific queries; demand is unavailable, not zero.
- Large measured municipal terms (`zoning`, `building permit`, `development applications`) prove the upstream research activity.
- The City of Toronto defines its neighbourhood “multiplex” permissions around 2–4 units, while CMHC multi-unit/MLI programs use a five-unit minimum. Searchers and marketers commonly blur this boundary.

## Required modules

- REQUIREMENT: Address/project intake decision tree.
- REQUIREMENT: 2–4 versus 5+ unit matrix covering municipal path, building form, financing category, valuation/rent, professional team, construction/draw needs, and takeout options.
- REQUIREMENT: Property-screening checklist: title/ownership, zoning, lot/built form, existing structure, services, trees/access, heritage, comparable applications, concept, unit mix, rents, budget, schedule, equity/liquidity, experience/team, exit.
- REQUIREMENT: “Before waiving conditions” evidence list.
- REQUIREMENT: Conversion versus addition versus teardown/new build comparison.
- REQUIREMENT: Link official municipal tools; do not turn preliminary screening into planning advice.
- REQUIREMENT: Route 5+ build-to-hold projects to MLI readiness and construction-to-takeout roadmap.
- REQUIREMENT: Route 2–4/garden/laneway projects to their distinct feasibility/financing resources.

## Answer targets

- Can I build or convert a multiplex on this Toronto property?
- What changes at five units?
- How do I estimate project cost, completed value, rents, and refinance proceeds?
- Can acquisition and construction be coordinated?
- Which professionals should be engaged before design and financing?
- What should be tested before making a firm offer?

## Required evidence and reviewers

- EVIDENCE: City of Toronto current multiplex and adding-units sources;
- EVIDENCE: CMHC current five-unit program boundary;
- REVIEW: planner/designer, code/engineering as required, valuation, construction budget, legal/tax, lender/underwriter, compliance;
- REFRESH: 90 days for planning, 30 days for program/economics.

## Acceptance criteria

- terminology boundary is explicit above the fold;
- no property-specific approval conclusion from public data alone;
- no unsupported development margin/rent/value claim;
- decision tree produces a transparent “next evidence required” result;
- CTA requests a property/project feasibility review and identifies the desired unit count/hold/sell strategy.
