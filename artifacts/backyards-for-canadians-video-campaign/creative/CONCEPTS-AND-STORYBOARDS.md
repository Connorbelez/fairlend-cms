# Concepts and Frame-Accurate Storyboards

## Campaign architecture

The campaign uses one master platform—**Backyards for Canadians**—with five distinct jobs:

| Territory | Job | Viral mechanism | Conversion bridge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Homes for neighbours, not tourists | Earn attention | Instant moral contrast | Property review |
| Canada needs hosts | Reframe identity | Pattern-interrupt wordplay | Host a household |
| Put your backyard to work | Create movement | Public pledge + visible progress | Join / review / share |
| Build wealth. Build housing. | Resolve economics | “Do well and do good” utility | Feasibility + financing |
| One backyard. One more Toronto home. | Prove locality | Block-level transformation | Case study / review |

The stakeholder film then demonstrates how all five territories operate as one measurable origination system.

## 01 — Homes for Neighbours, Not Tourists

**Format:** 15s, 9:16, HyperFrames  
**Audience:** Toronto homeowners considering short-term-rental income or underusing backyard land  
**Hook:** A suitcase enters as the familiar symbol of hosting; a lime route redirects it into a permanent front-door key.  
**Primary CTA:** `See what your backyard could become.`  
**Viral behaviour:** Moral contrast in the first second, but the payoff quickly becomes homeowner utility rather than scolding.

### Voiceover

> Toronto doesn’t need another weekend listing. It needs a front door for a neighbour. Your backyard could create one. FairLend. Build wealth. House a neighbour.

### Storyboard

| Time | Picture | Motion / framework leverage | Audio / copy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| 1.40–4.20 | Split file: left is a suitcase icon; right is a blank front-door outline. | Lime route line draws from suitcase to door; staggered block transition redirects the eye. | Caption highlights `WEEKEND LISTING`. |
| 4.20–7.40 | The route reaches the door; the blank door becomes an engraved garden-suite illustration. | SVG path trace, parallax paper layers, decisive key-turn stamp. | VO: “It needs a front door for a neighbour.” |
| 7.40–10.80 | Property-plan view: house, backyard lot, new suite footprint. | Blueprint lines draw synchronously; lot area receives a lime hatch. | VO: “Your backyard could create one.” |
| 10.80–13.40 | Headline fills frame: `HOMES FOR NEIGHBOURS / NOT TOURISTS.` | Editorial push transition; `NEIGHBOURS` arrives first, `TOURISTS` is struck through by the route. | VO brand resolve. |

### Cutdowns

- 6s bumper: suitcase → front-door key → campaign line.
- 1:1 feed: keep the split-file metaphor; reduce body copy.
- Static: baggage tag on left, legal front-door plaque on right.

## 03 — Put Your Backyard to Work

**Format:** 20s, 9:16, HyperFrames  
**Audience:** Civic-minded owners, neighbourhood advocates, founding homeowners  
**Hook:** A single backyard pledge becomes a visible Toronto-wide movement.  
**Primary CTA:** `Put yours on the map.`

### Voiceover

> A movement can start with one backyard. Pledge it. Review it. Finance it. Build it. Then put one more Toronto home on the map. Backyards for Canadians.

### Storyboard

| Time | Picture | Motion / framework leverage | Audio / copy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.00–2.60 | A paper lawn sign rises from a surveyed backyard: `I’M PUTTING MY BACKYARD TO WORK FOR TORONTO.` | Layered sign reveal, subtle 3D perspective, paper impact. | VO opening. |
| 2.60–6.60 | One lime point lands on a Toronto contour map; route lines branch to new points. | SVG route tracing and synchronized pin stamps; no fake live counts. | `ONE PLEDGE` then `A MOVEMENT`. |
| 6.60–11.80 | Stage rail advances: `PLEDGED → REVIEWED → FINANCED → BUILDING → HOME`. | File-card push transitions; each verified state uses a distinct stamp. | VO: “Pledge it. Review it. Finance it. Build it.” |
| 11.80–15.80 | Map pulls back; one highlighted parcel resolves into a front door. | GSAP camera-scale illusion and blueprint-to-engraving reveal. | “One more Toronto home.” |
| 15.80–20.00 | Master campaign line, CTA, disclosure. | Lime route completes a Toronto-shaped underline, final fade. | `BACKYARDS FOR CANADIANS` / `PUT YOURS ON THE MAP.` |

### Participation system

- Share card displays the homeowner’s chosen dedication, never an unverified completion claim.
- Dashboard always separates pledged, reviewed, financed, under-construction, and completed states.
- Physical lawn signs and site hoarding use the same stage rail and QR route.

## 05 — One Backyard. One More Toronto Home.

**Format:** 20s, 9:16, HyperFrames  
**Audience:** Neighbourhood-level owners who need tangible proof, not campaign rhetoric  
**Hook:** Empty space advances through five visible, verifiable states and becomes a front door.  
**Primary CTA:** `See what your property could become.`

### Voiceover

> Every new home begins as unused space. Then a plan. A permit. A build. A front door. One backyard becomes one more Toronto home. See what yours could become.

### Storyboard

| Time | Picture | Motion / framework leverage | Audio / copy |

### Proof rules

- Production case-study versions must use project-specific consent and the verified stage.
- `HOME +1` means the depicted narrative outcome, not an unverified aggregate campaign count.

## 06 — The Campaign in Motion (Stakeholder Film)

**Format:** 82s, 16:9, Remotion with embedded HyperFrames excerpts  
**Audience:** FairLend leadership, compliance, brokers, partners, and campaign stakeholders  
**Job:** Make the full brief feel like a coherent, exciting, measurable operating system—not a collection of slogans.  
**Primary decision:** Approve a 90-day pilot built around qualified property reviews and verified project progression.

### Voiceover

> Toronto has a housing problem hiding in plain sight: thousands of backyards with the potential to become legal, long-term homes. FairLend can turn that latent space into a movement—and a qualified origination engine.
>
> The platform is Backyards for Canadians. Its public provocation, Homes for neighbours, not tourists, earns attention in one second. The viral hook, Canada needs hosts—just not the Airbnb kind, reframes the homeowner as a city-builder.
>
> Then the campaign converts attention into action. Build wealth. Build housing. explains the owner economics without promising returns. One backyard. One more Toronto home. proves progress, block by block.
>
> Every channel has a job: film creates debate; paid social qualifies; search captures intent; calculators and property reviews create utility; signs, maps, and project milestones make participation visible.
>
> The funnel is simple: attention, identification, property review, financing, construction, advocacy. Success is not views. It is qualified reviews and real projects advancing through verified stages.
>
> FairLend coordinates property review, design, permits, builders, financing, draws, and takeout—so homeowners do not need to become developers.
>
> This is not charity. It is a credible way to do well and do good: dependable long-term income, less turnover, and one more home for Toronto.
>
> Launch with three films, six social units, founding homeowners, partner kits, and visible proof. Build wealth. House a neighbour. Put Toronto’s backyards to work.
