Mobile priority and behavior

  1. Headline and one-paragraph thesis.
  2. Each person’s name and current role.
  3. Discipline label and responsibility sentence.
  4. Optional proof line, visually subordinate and removable if density becomes excessive.
  5. Closing line and FAQ transition.

The desktop register is not implemented as an HTML table. Use semantic articles or a list with description groups so each row can compose vertically on mobile without losing headers or reading order.

Accessibility and interaction contract

  • Use a labelled <section> with one visible <h2>.
  • Render the roster as a semantic list of four articles or description groups; names are <h3> headings.
  • Do not rely on the lime line, row number, or spatial columns to communicate role or responsibility.
  • Keep body and responsibility copy at least 1rem with 4.5:1 contrast; metadata may be smaller only when nonessential.
  • Default content remains visible. Any entrance enhancement must preserve the complete reduced-motion and no-JavaScript reading experience.
  • No carousel, tabs, accordion, hover-only disclosure, or focusable decorative elements.

Section contract

Role / goal

Trust section; reduce key-person risk by showing named, complementary leadership responsibility.

Target belief

“I understand the experienced people and disciplines behind complex execution.”

Visual role

Calm responsibility ledger that completes the Leadership chapter and decompresses the page.

No interaction. Optional one-time rule draw or dossier settle; never pinned or scrubbed.

Desktop

Asymmetric proposition + wide ruled register. Content-height, paper dominant.

Tablet / mobile

Proposition first; rows compose vertically with intact names, roles, disciplines, and responsibilities.
